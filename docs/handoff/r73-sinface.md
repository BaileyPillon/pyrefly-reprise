## 3. The cause, link by link (what is proved, how, and what is not)

1. **The review run is at `fast` from Auron's Dragon Fang on, in all three runs.** *Proved from the review's own data*: Lulu's cast (ready>cast swap to cast>idle swap) lasts 160 frames in the treated run and 171 to 172 in the control and in live release 70 until frame 8552 / 8684 / 8919, then **52 frames** in all three (35 later), the 0.32 of every duration (`continuity.json` `swaps`, `critic/reviews/4c01379d4-focused` and `0804dc482-focused`). Auron's and Wakka's ready>attack to attack>follow gap is 30 to 33 (Auron) and 31 (Wakka) frames up to frames 8118 / 8297 and **exactly 10 from frame 9147 / 9224 on**; the control reads 6 to 7 in both halves. The house strike's own gap on the real actor is **29 frames at normal and 10 at 0.32** (`r73-sinface-trace`).
2. **At `fast` the house strike steps 0.461 of its reach in one frame (0.153 at normal), the way home 0.251 (0.081).** *Proved by running* the real strike under the real actor: head step = share x reach x drawn height / 1.82, to 8 percent, at 1.4, 3 and 4.1 units and 420 and 500 px, at both speeds.
3. **That reproduces the 17 hops.** *Proved by running*: 502 px drawn / 3.2 units steps 396 px at its travel peak (review 401.1, Auron's Dragon Fang), 422 / 2.6 steps 270 (278.3), 410 / 1.4 steps 145 (148.6); the real `detectJerks` calls each a jerk **in the frame the swap is made**, ratio 12 to 16 (the review: 4.1 to 18); at normal the same strikes read no jerk. Read the other way, the ten ready>attack hops imply a reach of **1.42 to 1.43** for Auron's four later ones (the house lunge, `StrikeReach` base 1.4), **2.6 to 3.2** for his first three (a solved reach, inside the cap of 4.4) and **1.2** for Wakka's three. At normal they would need 7.7 to 9.5 for the first three: the old lane's "one strike law is not all seventeen hops" was the wrong speed.
4. **Today's lunge ignores the speed**, so the control cannot hop. *Proved by reading and by test*: `startAttackMove` calls `lunge(distance, LUNGE_MS, contact, house)` with no scale and `strike({ timeScale: moveScale(ctx) })` (pinned); the lunge steps 51 to 142 px at either speed against 145 to 396 for the strike at `fast`, and its own jerk is 36 px at most (the review's control: none over 60; the six before frame 9148: 24.5 to 36).
5. **What set the speed to `fast`.** *By elimination plus the mechanism proved on the real `Input`, not yet observed in a browser.* The only callers of `setSpeed` / `fastForward` are R1 (`BattleScreenPause`), the demo attract, the debug API and its speed triggers, and the encounter options; the harness uses none of the last four. The Bushido sequence of Dragon Fang has an R1 chip and `run.json` shows the harness typed `r` for it with `page.keyboard.press` (keydown and keyup back to back). A keyboard tap that begins and ends between two frames leaves `Input` with `justPressed('r1')` true and `justReleased('r1')` never true (the pad's path got `pendingRelease` in release 64; the keyboard's `onKeyUp` never did), so `presenter.fastForward()` ran and `setSpeed('normal')` never did (`battle-fastforward-hold.test.ts`: the old two lines leave `fast` on the real `Input`; they cannot return).
6. **Why the earlier chapters did not show it**: only Chapter XVIII's fight has a Bushido (Dragon Fang) and so an R chip; the other 17 treated chapters never press R1 in the harness. (Not run: a search of the other chapters' sequences.)

*Still by reading, not by a run (rule 3), the suspects the first version listed*: `moments` (held, not playing), `smear` and the cut-anchor row (inert under the default scope; tested), `feel` (the camera punch and hit-stop move the camera and the field clock, never a plane), `fx` (particles and planes of their own), the HUD's stage rect, the SIN clock column (CSS), the camera `keepsToday` (empty plan), the real clips (held), the head lock (scales about the feet inside 0.9 to 1.1), the pose registration (feet within 1 px at these swaps). **An observation, not a cause**: the thirteen hops from frame 9147 on start their blend in the same frame as a camera cut (or one frame from it), the ten earlier attacks 2 or 3 frames after. That is the speed again (the whole presenter, camera included, ran 3.1 times faster), not a coupling of the strike's swap to the cut.

**Not matched, kept open** (pinned as OPEN in the test): (a) Wakka's reach reads 1.2 for his first three hops and 0.74 for his last three, under the 1.4 base; his lunge distance is untraced. (b) The seven attack>follow hops are 9 or 10 frames after their pair (matched) but about 1.5 times the size the way-home law gives (2.0 to 2.1 units where the pair reads 1.42; 4.1 to 4.4 where it reads 2.6 to 3.2); the harness swaps on `settlePeak`, the presenter's swap may fall on a different frame of the way home.
