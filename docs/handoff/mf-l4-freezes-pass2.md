## 1. MF-28, the garbage: the profile, the three allocators, the numbers

Method: Chromium's sampling heap profiler (`HeapProfiler.startSampling`, 32 KB interval, garbage included), 60 s of the game's own fight (the lean driver, seed 1, `?pack=demo`, 2560 x 1440, 60 Hz capped, headless GPU) from 12 s (Chapter IV) or 20 s (Chapter I) into the battle, folded through the build's hidden source maps by the first frame in `src/` (`pace/tools/heapfold.mjs`, `heapfold2.mjs`: the same fold with three's own frames named). The first number of each row is the first-pass tip (`ae02bf6bd`), the second this branch.

| | Chapter IV (FFX-2, Bahamut) | Chapter I (FFX, Seymour Flux) |
|---|---|---|
| allocation, 60 s window | 437 to 268 KB a frame (25.6 to 15.7 MB a second; 3,602 and 3,603 frames) | 299 to 240 KB a frame (17.2 to 13.8 MB a second; 3,544 and 3,536 frames) |
| three's program lookups a frame (10 s, 600 frames) | 38.64 to 6.77 | 13.65 to 0.59 |
| scavenges a minute (2-minute steady slice) | 108.5 to 67.0 | 74.0 to 59.5 (and heap drops of over 20 MB, the major collections: 7.5 to 2.0) |
| heap growth, MB a minute | 1,341 to 814 | 990 to 736 |

**What the profile said.** Half of Chapter IV's allocation (257 KB of 437 KB a frame) was inside three's `renderer.render` under `SsaaPass.ts:148`, and 47 percent of that (121 KB a frame) was one function, three's `getParameters`: the program lookup, a parameters object and a cache-key string made again for a material whose program had not changed. Two causes, both found by wrapping `Material.customProgramCacheKey` (one call per lookup) in the page (`pace/tools/probe-programs.mjs`, `probe-version.mjs`, `probe-why.mjs`):

1. **Chapter IV: three splits a transparent double-sided material into a back-face pass and a front-face pass,** flipping `material.side` and setting `needsUpdate` each time, and every flip is a lookup. The eight crossed light-shaft planes of Bevelle underground (six `light-shaft`, two `hole-shaft`: PlaneGeometry, additive, `depthWrite: false`) were drawn in the frame and again in the mirror floor's reflection: 8 x 2 passes x 2 flips = 32 lookups a frame. An additive plane with no depth write draws the same pixels in one pass (from the front it has no back faces; additive terms add in any order), so `forceSinglePass: true` on those two materials (`bevelle-underground.ts`, two lines, no line added to the 2,361-line file). **FFX-2 only.** Lookups 38.64 to 6.77 a frame (what is left is the mirror floor's nested render, which three gives its own light list, so the three lit materials look their program up in both passes: three's design).
2. **Chapters I and IV: the boss mask pass hid the lights.** `look/pro/BossMask.ts` draws the figures flat into a half-size target through a camera that sees only layer 6. Three keeps one render state, and so one light list and one light hash, for every render of a scene at the same depth, and lists only the lights the camera's layers can see: the mask pass saw none. The hash flipped between "five lights" and "none" twice a frame, every flip bumped `lights.state.version`, and every lit Lambert and Phong material (13 a frame in Chapter I) looked its program up again. `lightsOnLayer(scene, MASK_LAYER)` puts the scene's lights on layer 6 as well (scanned once every 30 frames); the mask pass draws flat with the shadow map off (`BossMask.draw` already set it), so lights in its list change nothing it draws. Lookups 13.65 to 0.59 a frame in Chapter I. **Both games** (the two re-cut bosses).
3. **The pro camera's `ndcRect`** made two arrays a corner (`ProFit.ts:37`, 16.7 KB a frame, 3.7 percent): scalars and an index now, the same arithmetic in the same order; `tests/unit/procam-ndc-exact.test.ts` holds it to the release-64 body on 4,000 random boxes and poses with `toBe`. Chapter IV 16.7 to 5.9 KB a frame. **Both games.**

**Look.** Identity is checked on the same page, not between builds (two builds differ by a 4-frame noise cycle and by timing): `l5/tools/toggle2.mjs` (the lane L5 capture library, not merged) takes the first command menu of each chapter on a virtual 60 Hz clock, and in each state runs 4 settle frames and 8 consecutive frames with the clock NOT advanced, hashing each screenshot. Chapter IV: shafts in two passes (`forceSinglePass` false) equal the one-pass base on 8 of 8 frames; lights off the mask layer equal it on 8 of 8; the positive controls differ on every frame (shafts hidden 0 of 8 equal, lit meshes hidden 0 of 8 equal), so the test does see a change. Chapter I: lights off the mask layer equal the base on 8 of 8; the lit-mesh control differs on every frame. (One transient: Chapter IV's second base state showed two distinct pictures among its 8 frames, one equal to the first base; the three later base states matched 8 of 8.) `ndcRect` is held to the release-64 body bit for bit by 4,000 random cases.

**Main-thread cost** (the recorder's rAF-callback time, steady slice 90 to 210 s): Chapter IV 3.68 ms (the first-pass tip) to 2.44 ms (mean of the rAF callbacks' time a frame; p95 5.4 to 3.9, p99 7.9 to 5.3; the build with the shafts and `ndcRect` alone read 2.13). Chapter I's two runs, hours apart on a PC the other lanes share, read 3.31 and 3.87, which is the machine and not the change (the same build moved 3.6 to 4.5 ms between its own 6 s windows), so the lights change was measured inside one session: 8 alternating 6 s windows with the lights on the mask layer and off (`pace.mjs --ab=lights`): window-mean JS ms a frame ON 4.36, 3.66, 7.28 (a spike window), 3.67; OFF 4.34, 4.21, 4.54, 4.09; medians 4.01 against 4.27. It costs nothing measurable; the lookups it removes are 13 a frame and the saving is a few tenths of a millisecond a frame, which this measurement is too noisy to resolve.

What the profile still shows (not changed here): three's uniform setters (`setValueM4`, `setValueV3f`) 40 to 55 KB a frame, inherent to its draw loop; the FFX HUD's `hudSafeZones.solveBox` and `freeSpans` (14 KB a frame in Chapter I: the advisor's placement solver runs on far more frames than its comment says; `FFXBattleHud.ts` is the HUD lane's file, left alone); `battle/ffx/intent.ts` `cloneCtx` (8 KB a frame, the enemy-intent dry run); `LookBold.figures` (6.5 KB).

## 2. MF-02, the worker where the browser cannot do the work

The first pass's worker was never run in WebKit. Playwright's WebKit (Windows) has `Worker` and `createImageBitmap` but **no `OffscreenCanvas`**: every painting was sent to a worker that could not draw it. The smoke (`pace/tools/webkit-smoke.mjs`, Chapter I, 60 s): **asked 318, landed 0, failed 106**, the page reading every painting for itself anyway (58 synchronous extents reads, 4 stand-ins), no page error. A browser without `OffscreenCanvas` is now left alone for good (no worker is made), and a worker that answers `ok: false` six times in a row with nothing ever landed is given up on; Safari 16.4 and newer, Chrome and Firefox have it and are unchanged. After: **asked 0, failed 0, no worker made (`broken` true), the battle reached and played, no page error, 52 synchronous extents reads** (the release-64 path). This is Playwright's WebKit for Windows; real Safari (16.4 and newer has `OffscreenCanvas` in workers) needs a Mac and the booth's own browsers, which this lane did not have. `tests/unit/alpha-thumb-giveup.test.ts` (3 tests).

## 4. What the CPU profile of Chapter I says is left (V8 sampling profile, 130 s, 0.5 ms samples, `pace/tools/cpufold.mjs`)

The main thread was busy 31.3 s of 130 s. **21 runs of 40 ms or more**; the first-sight class is now the larger part:

| class | runs | where |
|---|---|---|
| a painting's first draw into a 2D canvas (mip build of a 4x master) | 8 (60 to 374 ms, 1.16 s) | `Silhouette.alphaMaskFrom` at a strike's start (4: 129, 151, 167 ms and a 60 ms one); `PaintedMatte` and `PaintedArt.measureAlpha` when a master loads mid-fight (4: 141, 64, 77, 374 ms) |

The cost of the first class is **per image, once** (the first-pass E1: 79 to 202 ms for a 4x master the first time, 0.1 to 0.3 ms again): whichever reader draws that image first pays it. The first pass moved the extents off the page, so the payer is now the strike's silhouette or the load's matte. No worker, bitmap or smaller tier gives the page's bytes (17 variants, 0 identical), so the exact answer is to pay it where nobody sees it: **read the hot poses of the party, the aeons and the boss while the loading card and the cutscene are up** (batch 1's M-04 slices, `prebuildFigures`, which runs only under `?demo=1`), and to let the cold ones (sleep, victory, overdrive) read only when shown. That is a change of when, not of what, and it belongs with the opening's plan, not with this lane.

The steady CPU: **`FFXBattleHud.stageRect` is 27.8 percent of the busy time (8.7 s of 31.3 s, about 1.1 ms a frame in Chapter I)**, `hudScale` 1.3, `advisorChipSizes.measure` 2.2, `EnemyIntent.layout` 2.7, `solveAdvisorPlacement` 1.2: rect reads of the HUD (`getBoundingClientRect`, three per call, fifteen calls a solve, every frame) that force the style and layout the frame would do anyway, once per write-then-read. This is the MF-19 area (the HUD lane's file): a ResizeObserver cache of the panel rects, or one batch of reads a frame, would take a large share of 1.1 ms out of every frame, which is 16 percent of a 144 Hz frame; not changed here.
