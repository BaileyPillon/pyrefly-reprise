# r64-numbers: glass damage numbers that never overlap, and the Mega Flare menu rule checked (conference demo, 2026-10-24)

Branch `r64-numbers` from `origin/r63-play` (03116347d), tree `D:/pyrefly-anim-proto`, dev port 5380 (stopped). Nothing deploys. **Game case: BOTH games** for the numbers (the shared numeral layer `src/ui/common/DamageNumbers.ts`, which FFX's `ui/ffx/DamageNumbers.ts` and FFX-2's `ui/ffx2/DamageLayer.ts` both ride: FFX multi-target spells and Overdrives, FFX-2 multi-hit and multi-target moves alike), acting only where the glass numbers act (Chapter I and Chapter IV behind `?pack=demo` / `?look=pro`; every other build's numbers are untouched). **FFX-2 only** for the menu rule (D-357, D-441: the command menu is FFX-2's; FFX has none, and the new check reads the FFX-2 HUD's menu and is skipped for an FFX layer).

## 1. The defect: two glass numbers drawn on top of each other

What the driver saw (clip sheet `clips/ch1-sheet.jpg`, panel 5): during Seymour Flux's Total Annihilation, "778" printed over another number where two party members stand close.

**Reproduced first** (`numbers/tools/ch1.mjs`: headless Chromium at 2560x1440, `?demo=1&pack=demo`, Chapter I played to Total Annihilation on the virtual clock, every numeral's box sampled each frame, on the build before this change): **176 frame-pairs with two numerals' boxes intersecting**, worst 39,115 px2 (Tidus "2311" under Kimahri "2455"); the same frame as the clip, `docs/screenshots/r64-numbers/ch1-total-annihilation-BEFORE-778-over-1550.jpg`, shows "778" printed through "1550". These are the numbers the pro look ticks up over a multi-hit (789, 1550, 2311 and 778, 1618, 2455): Tidus and Kimahri project to almost the same x, and the layer's per-target lanes and fan (`damageLadder.ts`) work per target, in logical pixels, before the pop and the HUD dodge, so they cannot keep two actors' wide, growing figures apart.

**The fix**: a last pass in SCREEN space over the glass numbers' boxes as they are about to be drawn.

- `src/ui/common/numberSpacing.ts` (new, 176 lines, pure: no DOM, no `three`, no clock, no randomness): `planNumberSpacing(items, {bounds, panels})`.
- Two numbers that BEGAN to show within 400 ms of each other (the presenter's beat is 190 to 275 ms a blow) and whose boxes would intersect (a gap of 16 percent of the shorter height, at least 4 px): the LATER one is moved. **Up first** (the way every numeral already climbs), then to the side with more room, then down. One refinement of "up first": a number whose target stands clearly BELOW the figure it meets (by more than 0.35 of its height) is moved down instead, so it never hops over that figure and is never read as the other actor's.
- Each number keeps its own target: the shift is added to the position its target's projection gave it, and is limited to 3.2 box heights up / 2.2 box widths sideways. A moved number stays inside the layer's bounds and out of the HUD panels (unless its own target is under the HUD, as the layer already treats it).
- **Sticky**: the layer keeps each number's shift on the numeral and hands it back, and the planner only ever adds to it. So nothing jumps when the figure a number made way for fades out and is dropped, and a pair never swaps sides in flight; the shift grows continuously from nothing as two boxes start to meet.
- Wired in `DamageNumbers.update` only for the glass numerals (`n.pro`): the placement loop collects them, `spaceAndPaint` plans and writes them; the old numerals are written exactly as before (`paint`). A moved numeral carries `data-spaced="up|down|left|right"`. Look, size, pop, rise, fade and timing are untouched; the glint and the FFX-2 CHAIN chip follow the moved figure (the chip reads the numeral's drawn box).

**Tests**: `tests/unit/number-spacing.test.ts` (22: the rules, the window, up/down/side, bounds, panels, stickiness, continuity over a flight, purity) and `tests/unit/number-spacing-layer.test.ts` (7: the layer in jsdom with the glass numbers on; FFX Total Annihilation as the driver saw it, with three rounds ticking up; FFX-2 multi-hit on two close targets with a heal beside; a worst case of two targets on one point; the old numerals untouched with the piece off). **Checked that they bite**: with the planner call replaced by nothing, 5 of the 7 layer tests fail.

**After** (same steps, `numbers/tools/ch1.mjs after`): the same fight (same party HP, same blows), numerals seen on the same 406 frames, **0 overlapping pair-frames** (was 176). `ch1-total-annihilation-AFTER-same-frame.jpg` is the same frame as the clip: "778" above "1550", "566" clear. The distribution of per-frame movement of every numeral is the same as before the change (the large steps in both runs are the battle camera's cuts, which re-project every numeral; none is new).

## 2. The check: Mega Flare, the held-breath wash and the command menu (FFX-2, Chapter IV)

The rule (D-357, D-441, `BreathPlan.ts`: with a command menu OPEN the wash, the HUD sink, the flood and the flash are held off; the name, the ring and the core still play; `menuHold` eases over 260 ms each way) was checked in the dev server at 2560x1440 on the virtual clock, every frame sampled (the layer's own frame: wash `drain`, `hud`, `flood`, `flash`, `menuHold`; the HUD's menu: `hidden`, rows, effective opacity; the presenter's `pendingMenu`). Tools: `numbers/tools/ch4.mjs`, `ch4m.mjs`, `analyse4*.mjs` (scratch).

**Finding: the rule HOLDS for a menu the player answers; it did NOT hold for a menu that stays on screen after the presenter has stopped waiting on it. That second case is what the clip frame shows, and it is fixed.**

| window (Chapter IV, Mega Flare) | what the menu did | wash, sink, flood, flash while a menu was on screen |
|---|---|---|
| natural, BEFORE the fix (the clip lane's way: a bot answers each menu 12 frames after it opens, by `pendingMenu.resolve`) | Paine's menu is answered, the presenter's `pendingMenu` goes null, **the HUD's own menu stays standing** (its promise is abandoned: `hidden` stays false, rows at full opacity) until the next menu opens | **171 frames** with the menu on screen and the wash on (drain 1, flood 1, flash 0.84, HUD sunk to 0.167): the clip's panel 5 (`ch4-mega-flare-BEFORE-stale-menu-under-the-wash.jpg`: Paine's full menu under the Mega Flare wash) |
| open (after the fix; a girl's menu held open through the whole breath) | open as the tell begins, through the blow and the lift | 0 frames; wash, sink, flood, flash all 0; the name, ring and core play |
| keysA, keysB (after; held, then answered by REAL KEY PRESSES through the HUD, 2 to 3 Enter presses; the ATB opens the next girl's menu before the tell) | the HUD hides its own menu at once; the next girl's menu is open at the tell and the blow | 0 frames; the wash never rose over a menu |
| quietK (after; real keys answer every menu that opens in the last seconds, the last one 1.5 s before the tell, no menu until after the blow) | closed, `hidden` true, `menuHold` eased to 0 | 0 frames with a menu; **the breath plays in full** once no menu is up (wash peak 1, flood 1, flash 0.84, HUD sunk to 0.167; `ch4-mega-flare-AFTER-menu-closed-wash-plays.jpg`) |
| quietS (after; the same, but answered over the HUD with `pm.resolve`, so the HUD's menu is left standing) | stays on screen through the breath | 0 frames; wash, sink, flood, flash all 0 (`ch4-mega-flare-AFTER-menu-up-no-wash.jpg`: the menu in full, the core and the name playing) |

**Why**: the layer's menu probe is the presenter's `menuBlocks(ctx)` = `pendingMenu !== null`. A player's answer goes through the HUD (`chooseCommand` resolves and the HUD hides its menu in the same call), so the two agree. An answer that comes OVER the HUD (`setAutoPlay`, `__pyrefly.autoBattle`, a recorder's `pendingMenu.resolve`: the debug API and a recorder use it; the e2e/critic flow and the booth attract loop run the auto strategy from the start, which never opens a HUD menu at all, and a booth player answers through the HUD) clears `pendingMenu` but leaves the HUD's menu standing, and the layer then played the wash over a menu still on screen. **Fix (in the staging layer, FFX-2 only)**: `ProStageLayer.menuOnScreen()` also treats a visible `.ffx2hud__command` with rows as an open menu: asked once as the tell begins (the wash never starts) and each frame while a breath is telling (read only then: it reads layout). FFX has no menu rule and its layer never looks. Test: `tests/unit/engine/pro-staging-ui.test.ts` (+1; it fails with the check removed).

**For recorders**: a clip that wants the wash must close the menu the way a player does (real keys, or `__pyrefly.battle().hud.closeCommandMenu()`); a menu left standing now holds the wash off, exactly as it would for a person with a menu up.

**Not changed, and worth knowing**: a menu that opens MID-breath (a girl's gauge fills during the tell) still eases the wash out over 260 ms (`MENU_EASE_MS`, chosen in review so nothing pops), so for up to a quarter second a fading wash and a fading-in menu are both on screen. No window in these runs opened a menu inside the tell (the ATB opened them before it), so it was not measured live; the unit test covers the ease. Say if it should be shorter (for example 120 ms) at the price of a harder cut.
