## 0. What changed against the preflight (read first)

1. **The build is the preflight, as written; one real piece of invention, flagged:** the Luca party (section 4). The note's "Threaten lands" is corrected (below), and one finding about the balls is new (item 3).
2. **Threaten fails on both** (the note read the byte 0 as "lands"; this repository's reading of a monster's Threaten byte is a success percentage, so 0 is immune, which is what Samurai Gamers says). Wendigo's byte is 100 and lands; the Guado Guardian's is 0 and does not. The data says `threatenChance: 0`; the note has a section 10 with the correction (`research/re-ffx-oblitzerator-crane.md`).
3. **FINDING, then FIXED in the fix pass (section 13.1): the engine ran a ball before the swinger's end-of-turn tick, so the Blind and Mute Ball's status did not outlast the swing; the game runs it after the tick.** The balls inflict Darkness, Silence and Sleep at chance 254 with duration code 1 (the record's own). This build's first reading of the engine (`engine-end.ts#afterAction`: the reaction drain, then `onTurnEnd`) put the status off with the very turn that set it off, which left only the ball's damage, with the cures dead weight. The guides advise the cures, which suggested the game orders it the other way; the review called it the one MAJOR and the fix pass read the order in the game's executable: the hits are applied, the script's reaction is queued behind the attacker's own entry and the attacker's end-of-turn tick runs in the same pass, so the **ball runs after the tick and its Darkness or Silence lasts through the victim's next turn**. The ball is now queued `afterTick` (an opt-in flag; no other script's order changed).
4. **The Doze Ball is unreachable, proved four ways** (section 3).
5. **The crane line costs one Rush and nobody falls** (section 6): 13 decisions, 1.00 Rush, 0.00 balls, 0.00 KOs, 200 of 200, on two seed sets, at the shipped build and at the raw start records. The slog (no crane, with care) also wins 200 of 200 at the shipped build, in 31 decisions and 6.8 Rushes; the preflight predicted it would be hard, and it is not, at stop 3. At the raw start records it wins 41 percent.
6. **The move card does not follow the crane line** (section 11, open issue 2): the guide's NEXT line, the auto-battler and the tactic do; the card's top row is "Thunder -> Oblitzerator" (the slog), because the card follows a chapter's line only for the three chapters in `advisor-line.ts`.

## 2. What it is

Six thousand HP, no Defense, **weak to Thunder** (x3/2), resists Fire, Ice, Water and Holy, not Armored. One move of its own and three reflexes; and a crane.

| What happens | What it does (the game's own scripts, RE 4) |
|---|---|
| its turn (AGI 1: once per about **5.8** of the party's actions) | **Blitzball Rush**: ten balls of power 4, **each at a member picked at random** over the living front line, 32 to 39 apiece at this build; **neither Protect, Shell nor Defend halves them** (damage type 0); a Cheer does. Nothing else, ever |
| it is hit by a command whose formula byte is **1** (a weapon blow, the physical skills, the Breaks, Spiral Cut) | **Blind Ball** on the attacker, one time in three (`GetRandomValue() mod 3 == 0`: **21,846 of 65,536**); it runs after his turn's end tick, so the Darkness lasts through his next turn |
| it is hit by a command whose formula byte is **3** (any spell, Lancet, Scan, a Fury, the status spells) | **Mute Ball** on the attacker, one time in three (the Silence lasts through her next turn) |
| formula byte 2 | **Doze Ball**: no player command has one |
| its HP falls under a third (strictly under 2,000) | AGI + 15 (1 to 16): its turn comes every **36** ticks instead of 84; under a twelfth (under 500) AGI + 10 more (26: 27 ticks); one step per hit |
| **the Crane row names it** | no more turns, no more counters (and the Crane row takes **15/16 of its current HP**) |
| the **crane** (65,535 HP, no turns, off the CTB bar, no part of the victory, absorbs Thunder, immune to the rest) | the first two Thunder casts (the player's own row 0x3043) are answered with a line; **the third powers it**; one **Thunder Fury** powers it at once; Thundara, Thundaga, the other Furies, Fire and a weapon do nothing |
| **Use crane** (Tidus's Trigger Command, **the first row of his menu**, from the start) | on the dark crane: a line, nothing else, the turn spent; on the powered crane: the Crane row on the machine (**5,625 off 6,000, 375 left**), the command leaves his menu, the crane cannot be named again |

Rewards: **580 gil, 36 AP (54 on an overkill of 600), an Elixir** (a certain drop), a Potion to steal (both rolls); the gear drop is not modelled (section 3). **The design fact:** the first-time player is meant to read the crane, not out-damage 6,000 HP; a fan who ignores it can still win.

Every number is `research/re-ffx-oblitzerator-crane.md` (the game's own files: monster files `m110` and `m133`, formation `cdsp02_00`, the Atel scripts run in the earlier lanes' interpreter; "RE n" in the files), tagged `[bytes]`, `[run]`, `[arith]`. **The Doze Ball is unreachable, four ways:** (1) the six rows of the kernel fixture with formula 2 are all monster moves (ids from 0x4000); (2) `formulaByteOf` reads no ability of the party's (above 300 of them) as 2; (3) no Overdrive is 2 (the physical ones are 1, the Furies 3); (4) a synthetic row of formula 2 does draw the Doze Ball (the branch is in the code and the data). Two guides say an Overdrive draws it; the files win; **live check (RE 8, item 1)**.

**What that says, plainly.**
1. **The prediction held for the crane line** (5 to 7 party actions, one Rush, nobody falls): it wins every fight on both seed sets and on both builds, in 11 to 13 decisions, and the Haste on Lulu only makes it 4 decisions shorter. The target (95 percent) is met with room, and no lever was needed. **The fix did not touch it**: the crane line never swings at the machine while the crane is dark, so no ball is ever drawn.
2. **The slog is no longer a free win.** With the balls' statuses on (the game's order), a Mute Ball takes Lulu's Thunder away for a turn and a Blind Ball spoils the swinger's next blow: the slog without cures wins 94 percent at the shipped build and 17 percent at the raw start records; with the cures the guides advise it wins 99 and 25.5 percent. **Without care or the Thunder it loses nearly every fight** (naive 3 percent, careless 0, no Thunder 1 to 2.5 percent). The preflight guessed the slog would be hard; at the shipped build it is a long but winnable fight with care, as the guides say.
3. **The only way the crane line fails is not waking it:** Fire, Blizzard or Water at the crane do nothing, the line never gets its Use crane, and the party Defends into 14 Rushes (0 of 200).
4. **A fight takes 0.03 s of engine time.** The bench takes about 2 minutes for 200 seeds of ten lines.

**Disclosed, not nerfed.** The fight is the game's tutorial: an informed player wins it with one Rush taken. A player who ignores the crane wins about 94 to 99 percent of the time if he heals and cures, and almost never if he does not. Nothing was tuned; if Bailey wants it harder, the levers are in section 4.

### 13.1 The MAJOR (M1), fixed: a ball's status now lasts through the swinger's next turn

- **What the review reproduced:** on 4 of 4 counters Darkness was added, ticked and removed inside the swinger's own action. Only the 7 to 10 power damage was left of a ball, so the Eye Drops and Echo Screens were dead weight and the slog's numbers were for an easier machine than the game's.
- **How it was settled (rule 6, from the game's executable, not from a guide):** the repository's private decompile of FFX.exe (build 25501027) has the whole path. `pp_BtlApplyResults` (VA 0x0078d980) applies the hit records of the action, one target at a time (`pp_BtlApplyHitRecords`, VA 0x0078f060); when the last record of a target is applied it raises the script's `onHit` event (`pp_BtlActionRequest`, VA 0x007aceb0, slot 3). The command the script asks for is **queued, not run there**: `FUN_007929b0` calls `pp_BtlQueueAction` (VA 0x007b2300), which inserts it as a free entry (flag 1) **right behind the attacker's own queue entry**. In the same pass `ApplyResults` calls the attacker's `pp_BtlActionDone` (VA 0x007b20e0): it removes the attacker's entry and, for an entry of flag 0, adds the recovery and runs the end-of-turn status tick (VA 0x007af390). The queued ball is the next entry and runs afterwards. A status of duration code 1 is therefore counted off by the end-of-turn tick of the attacker's **next** turn. The infliction kernel's self-cast rule (a finite duration cast on oneself lasts a turn more, `research/re-ffx-ctb-status.md` section 15.1 S7) compares the two ids only, so it does not apply to a monster's ball. The note's section 10 holds the same reading with the confidence tags; the guides' advice to cure the statuses agrees.
- **The fix (FP1, below):** a reaction can be queued `afterTick`. `ai/hooks.ts` (`Reaction.afterTick`, `queueReaction`'s option, `takeReaction(ctx, skipAfterTick)`, `isAfterTick`, `hasAfterTickReactions`), `ai/reaction-drain.ts` (`drainScriptReactions(h, beforeTick)`), `ai/hit-script.ts` (`Counter.afterTick`), `ai/hit-gates.ts` (`react(..., afterTick)`), `engine-end.ts` (the two drains before the tick pass `beforeTick = true`; the existing drain after the tick runs the late ones; an extra `runItemReactions` only when a late reaction was queued), and `ai/oblitzerator.ts` (the balls pass `true`).
