## 1. Play it

1. From the title, reach the chapter-select board. Type **`duelist`** (any case, letters at most 2 s apart; the board shows no hint). It opens the chapter's party prep itself, with no card and no sound before it. **The first letter D is the board's `right`, which a first letter cannot claim, so the cursor takes one step** (as the `daigoro` and `darkaeon` doors do); E (`start`) and S (`down`) are later letters of a live match and are claimed by the word; U, L, I and T are bound to nothing, so the last letter leaves no press latched. On a phone, the long-press field on the "Chapter select" label takes the same word.
2. Enter starts the chapter: a pre scene (our draft, section 8), then **duel 1: Auron**. After his fall a seam of 12.8 seconds (the arena mends the girls), then **duel 2: the masked duelist** (the HUD shows "????"), a second seam of 11.0 seconds, **duel 3: Seymour**, then the post scene (which names the hidden one) and the results page. Links 2 and 3 open at full HP and MP with a fallen girl standing and **no Save Sphere card**; items spent stay spent; statuses and the worn dressphere do not carry. A defeat on duel 2 or 3 retries **that duel**; a defeat on duel 1 starts the chapter over. Escape is off.
3. A clear goes to the experiments' own store (`pyrefly-reprise:experiments:v1`), never the save: no save change, no migration. The party is Chapter XIII's Lv 99 three (Yuna and Paine Dark Knights, Rikku an Alchemist).

## 3. The data and its sources (rule 6: nothing invented)

`tests/unit/chapters/farplane-cup-data.test.ts` (72 tests) compares the enemy data, the monster records and the command records to the fixture field by field, and the rows the repo already holds under other names (Demi, Curaga, Dispel, Shell, Protect, Break, Confuse, Black Sky, Mega-Potion, X-Potion, Hero Drink, Magic Up) to these. **Rule 5 (hit rule):** `canMiss` follows each row's accuracy formula (misc word bits 3 to 5): `true` for the three Attacks, Full Break, Delay Buster, Shin Zantetsu, Drain Attack and Shooting Star (formula 3); `false` for every other move, set explicitly.

**Gaps, named (not invented):**
- **Item ids.** The files give ids the repo can pair with a name for the common drops and the steals (Mega Phoenix 0x2007, X-Potion 0x2002, Turbo Ether 0x2005, Hi-Potion 0x2001, Elixir 0x2008); the three rare drops (Bushido, Sword and Arcane Lore, 0x9063, 0x9062, 0x9064) have no item in the repo's tables, so those slots are 0 in the kill-reward rows (the raw ids are kept in the fixture). Bribe slots are laid for Seymour (Crystal Ball, Soul of Thamasa) though his Bribe bit is clear.
- **The cup itself.** No formation row and no launcher is in the files (RE 5): rounds, rewards and recruiting are not sourced and **not built** (DU1, DU8). The cup's other fighters (Brother, Buddy, Kimahri, Lulu, Baralai, Nooj, Gippal and the rest of rows 308 to 327) are not in this chapter (DU7).
- **Positions.** The near-weighted target search needs where everyone stands; the cup ships none, so `CUP_SCENE` (Trema's scene, the same battle-map family; the Maesters' stand-in) is one named constant, `[inferred]`. The alternative is `searchUniform`, a one-line change.
- **`stat_def_death`** (Auron's Shooting Star gate): read as the girl's Death resist byte (`[I]`). The Lv 99 build has none, so his low-HP roll 0 is Shin Zantetsu, which cannot land (T0 a).
- **An Eject guard for the girls.** The sources do give one (a Special-dressphere wearer cannot be Ejected, `research/ffx2-combat-core.md` sections 2.8 and 3.15) but the engine does not apply it; no boss row depends on it. Section 6 has the numbers.
- **Action time and stature** (animation length, model height) are unread; the Den of Woe stage's default foe height (2.10 against the girls' 1.78) is the stand-in.

### T0 findings (each pinned by a test in `farplane-cup-engine.test.ts`; the numbers are what the engine produced)

1. **(a) The status layer cannot touch a Lv 99 party except at chance 254 or 255.** The status roll lands when `roll < chance + 5 x (attacker level - target level) - resist` (`kernel/statusGroup1.ts`, the game's own rule), and the duelists are Lv 67 to 74. Shin Zantetsu's Death (80), Full Break's four stat drops (100), Break's Petrify (80), Confuse (100) and Slowga's Slow (130) **can never land on the Lv 99 girls**, even with no Luck (0 of 20 seeds each); the same rows land on a Lv 60 or Lv 74 girl (pinned with the girls' level lowered by the test, a rig). Only Shooting Star's Eject (254) lands: **20 of 20 seeds**, and a girl whose Eject resist byte is 255 is never ejected.
2. **(b) Luck.** The girls wear Rabite's Foot (Luck 111): every physical move that rolls (the three Attacks, Full Break, Delay Buster, Drain Attack; accuracy formula 2) is evaded nearly always (Auron's ACC 112: about 1 in 20; Tidus's ACC 139: 8 of 20 for Delay Buster); a bare party takes every one.
3. **Shooting Star is the fight.** Formula 3 (the resist-byte race) lands at 20 of 20; an ejected girl is out of the fight (no item raises her), about 2.4 girls a fight, which is why duel 1 is the hard one. (The sources' guard, a Special dressphere, is not applied by the engine: section 6.)
4. **The absorbs work.** A Fire spell cast by a Black Mage Yuna heals him (negative damage, affinity "absorb"); Paine's Demi (Gravity) heals him by the cap, 9,999.
5. **Haste and Invincible are his own statuses** (Hastega 40 counts, the Hero Drink 20); he re-hastes about three times a fight.
6. **The percent formulas do nothing to Auron and Tidus** (special bit 0, pinned: Paine's Demi takes nothing from either); Seymour's absorb of Gravity turns the same Demi into a heal of up to 9,999. **Tidus's Reflect immunity is data only** (the byte is 255 and the hit kernel treats 255 as immune): the party has no legal way to cast Reflect on an enemy, so no engine test of it exists.
7. **Blitz Ace** is nine real hits (each over 500, together over Rikku's 5,106 HP); at Rikku's real HP it puts her down.

## 6. Difficulty: measured, not tuned (rule 15)

`tests/unit/chapters/farplane-cup-bench.test.ts` (9 tests, 100 seeds by default, `PYREFLY_BENCH_SEEDS=<n>` for more; seeds 1 to 100, the engine deterministic, so the table is exactly reproducible), the harness `tests/unit/helpers/farplaneCupDrive.ts`. A *line* is a bot standing in for a player: `curtains` (Rikku's first two turns are a Light and a Lunar Curtain, then she mends with a Megalixir below half or a Phoenix Down on a fallen girl; Yuna and Paine cast Darkness), `darkness` (the same without the curtains), `charon` (Charon instead of Darkness), `walk` (Paine first crosses The End's red and green gates, four Changes, then Charon), `remedy`, `defend`. **No boss row was touched and the build is Chapter XIII's as given.**

(wins of 100 seeds a cell.) **What that says, plainly.** (a) **Duel 1 is the hard one: about one seed in nine.** Auron ejects 2.4 of the three girls a fight (Shooting Star lands every time and nothing raises an ejected girl), so the fight has to be won in about a minute; the chain from the start is ~11 x 45 x 54 percent = about 2.7 percent for the simplest bot, but a defeat on duels 2 and 3 retries only that duel, and duel 1 is a retry of itself. (b) **Duel 2** is the fast one: he opens with nine hits on one girl (a KO for any of them; 2.8 KOs a fight on the curtains line) and Rikku's Phoenix Down is what keeps the party up. (c) **Duel 3** is the easiest of the three: **his status spells (Break, Confuse, Slowga, Shin Zantetsu-class) cannot land on a Lv 99 party** (T0 a), so only Meteor, Black Sky, Ultima and Demi matter. (d) The `walk` and `charon` lines lose to the plain ones: Charon costs its caster HP and the walk spends the opening on Changes.

So **Shooting Star is the whole of duel 1's difficulty**: with the Eject switched off the same bot wins 95 of 100; a lower-level party is the harder fight on every duel, not the easier.

**The Eject guard (corrected in the review-fix pass, 2026-11-06; the first version of this note said no guard is sourced, which was wrong).** The repo's own research gives one: **a girl in a Special dressphere cannot be Ejected** (`research/ffx2-combat-core.md` section 2.8, the Eject row; and section 3.15, the Special Dress Up table, "Every SDSP part ... cannot be Ejected"; `[verified: 2 sources]`), and the build owns all three Specials (Floral Fallal on Yuna, Machina Maw on Rikku, Full Throttle on Paine: `builds/via-infinito.ts`). So the "girls Eject-proof" row above is the sourced rule applied as the engine would apply it if it did (a resist byte of 255), and it is the safest candidate for Bailey if he wants duel 1 reachable. Three facts a decision needs:

1. **The engine does not enforce it.** `src/battle/ffx2/dresspheres.ts` and `spherechange.ts` state the rule in comments only; nothing sets the Eject byte of a Special wearer (a party member's resists come from her accessories alone, `setup.ts accessoryImmunities`). Probe (kept outside the repo): Yuna with `dresspheres.current = 'floral-fallal'` is ejected by Shooting Star on 20 of 20 seeds, as in her normal form. So the row is a rig, not something a player can do today. Making it true is one rule in the status resist read (`adapt/inputs.ts hitTarget` for the formula-3 race, `words.ts resistTables` and `resolve-status.ts` for the status layer): **shared FFX-2 engine plumbing, not changed in this lane** (it touches every FFX-2 chapter where an enemy ejects, the experimental Leblanc's included); the driver's or Bailey's call.
2. **The road to a Special form is long.** Special Dress Up needs every node occupied and every dressphere on the grid worn this battle (`garment-grids.ts canSpecialDressUp`). The grids are Tempered Will (5 nodes, Yuna), First Steps (6, Rikku) and The End (5, Paine; the current dressphere counts as worn), so at least four Changes for each Dark Knight and five for Rikku, and a Special form has no Item command. Auron's first Shooting Star starts at a median of 18.2 game seconds (10th percentile 1.0 s, 90th 50.3 s; 100 seeds, curtains line; the girls have taken a median of 9 decisions by then: each acts about every 6 s), so a four-Change walk finishes after the median first Star, and the `walk` line above (four Changes) already measures what such a walk costs: 4 wins of 100.
3. **Not measured: the Special route itself** (a line that walks every girl's grid to Special Dress Up), because fact 1 makes the result depend on an engine rule that does not exist yet; with the rule added, the Eject-proof row is its upper bound and the walk's cost is fact 2.

Also not measured here: the Gunner chain and Warrior lines, a delay-lock on Auron (he is immune to Interrupt and delay: special bits 6 and 9), Reflect-based lines (Shooting Star and Meteor are not reflectable), Auto-Life. The lines, not the data, are the likely lever.
