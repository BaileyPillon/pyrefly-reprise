## The short answer

- **The phone's wait is the line, and the load diet did not touch it.** Release 68's focused review timed the phone profile (390 x 844, 10 Mbit/s, CPU x4): the load wait before the boss card was **19.8 s in Chapter I and 42.4 s in Chapter IV against 8 s** (live release 67: 20.1 and 39.2). The wire never rests: 87 MB after the press at 1.25 MB/s is 70 s, and the first command came at 69.5 s. Everything the fight start asks for shares the line with the few megabytes the entry needs, so the entry finishes when the line has drained. The diet changed what the first menu *waits for*, not what is *fetched* or in what order it competes.
- **The cut (section 2, change 2): foreground first on the phone class.** From `BattleScreen.enter` until `PaintedStage.stage` resolves (every figure is built) the lighting maps (17.9 MB in Chapter I, 13.7 in Chapter IV), a figure's later poses and the battle preload's phases 4 and 5 wait; then they start through a ranked queue, eight at a time across every figure, the poses a fight draws first first. **The same bytes in another order**; nothing changes on any other class. Prediction: Chapter I 19.8 s to about 8 s (central; 45 percent chance of 8.0 s or under; plausible, the gap between what the entry consumed and what it needs is the 13 MB of lighting maps), Chapter IV 42.4 s to about 12 s (**a subtraction, not a measurement**: the entry consumed about 51 MB of line against about 10 MB it needs, and the cut gates only the maps, the preload's rest and the later poses, not the audio (16.6 MB) nor the requests already in flight at `begin`; the CPU floor alone is 7.6 s and nothing here touches it). **B2a for the phone in Chapter IV will still FAIL, and B3a (bytes) is untouched: the bytes and the CPU are the limit** (section 3).
- **The cut moves the missing-art window into the fight (said plainly; the first version of this note called that cost "unchanged", which was wrong).** In release 68 the first command arrives when the line has nearly finished, so everything has landed. The cut sends the same bytes in another order, so the time a player spends in a fight with later poses and maps still on the wire equals the time the cut saves: about 12 s in Chapter I (about 15 MB) and about 29 s in Chapter IV (about 36 MB, audio included) at the central predictions (rows B2a-12 to B2a-15). For that long some attack, cast or hurt move draws the idle painting and some figures are unlit; FFX-2's Active mode puts enemy moves in the first seconds. **That is Bailey's look call (rule 9) and it needs those numbers first** (section 8, question 1).
- **The memory a fight leaves behind (change 1).** The same chapter five times in a row is flat; Chapter I and IV alternating climb for eight visits and then plateau (renderer +74 percent over four changes in the candidate, JS heap +7 MB, DOM flat after the FFX-2 HUD fix). The hypothesis (the lane said about even odds; **the second review says about one in five**): the page keeps the chapter before the one it is playing in two caches built "for a retry" (the decoded paintings, 96 of them; the lighting maps, 56 or 112). A fight is now an epoch; when its stage is disposed what the fight did not touch is dropped, so a retry is as warm as before and the other chapter is not kept. **That explains one step of about 150 MB, not the ramp**: the lane's own control arm reads 12, 24, 24, 24, 24 (one step, then flat) where the browser reads six steps, every revisit adds +88 to +127 MB at entry and leaving a fight returns only 8 to 32 MB (section 1.2), and the release leaves the in-fight peak unchanged while Windows private bytes follow committed allocator memory and so the peak. **Make b2-5 the first browser entry, read the books before the counters, and hold the first CHANGELOG line until it has run** (the decision rule is in section 5). The FFX HUD is proved flat by a twin of the FFX-2 release test.
- **Mutation checks (section 6):** the lane's 28 (26 killed by test name, 1 equivalent, 1 killed only by vitest's exit code on an unhandled rejection, now by name), the fix-up's 25 (23 killed, the 2 survivors are single-site equivalents that the double mutation kills), pass 1's 52 (11 survived) and pass 2's 15 (8 survived); **every non-equivalent survivor of both reviews is now killed by a test** (the equivalent ones, M19, X21 and X23, stay). The 7 new test files hold 76 tests and pass; `tsc` clean; no source file grew past 400 lines. The unit suite without the chapters' engine simulations passed before the fix-up (1,548 files, 24,786 tests); after it the related files were run (section 6).

## 1. What the evidence says

### 1.1 The phone

Source: `critic/reviews/2a9340249-focused/budgets/first-command-phone10-pad-{cand68,r67}.json` (read only), the r68 measuring lanes' `RESULTS.md` and `notes.md`, `docs/handoff/load-diet.md`, the code.

| | Chapter I (FFX) | Chapter IV (FFX-2) |
|---|---|---|
| load wait (entry-load + first-draw), candidate / live r67 | 19.8 s / 20.1 s | 42.4 s / 39.2 s |
| the split, candidate | prep 1.6, scene 39.9, **entry-load 18.3**, first-draw 1.5, card 1.3, sweep 6.4 (total 69.5) | scene **19.0 s** (19.8 s live r67; the desktop reads 17.7 s) of a total of 72.0 (the first version of this note said 15.9 s, which is in neither file) |
| wire bytes to the menu / after the press | 93.6 / 87.3 MB (images 69.4, audio 17.7) | 94.0 / 87.8 MB |
| no network limit (phone4x, measuring lane q4) | 4.2 s | 7.6 s (main thread busy 60 to 65 percent) |
| desktop 50 Mbit/s, candidate / r67 | 5.9 s / 6.0 s (110 MB) | 4.4 s / 4.5 s (112 MB) |

- **The line is busy for the whole run.** 87.3 MB at 10 Mbit/s (1.25 MB/s) is 69.8 s; the first command is at 69.5 s. The phone does not wait for 19 MB of "the first menu"; it waits for the transfer.
- **Why the diet did nothing here.** It lets a figure be built once its opening poses are in and streams the rest, but (its own handoff, M2) it does not change what is fetched: the 28 MB of other poses, the 17.9 MB of maps, the board's rest and the audio all still ask for the same 1.25 MB/s, and everything in flight shares the line equally. The entry's own loads (the boss's rig, 109 files; the opening poses of any figure the preload had not finished; the backdrop) get their share of a line that has 100 requests on it.
- **What shares it at the entry, from the code** (`BattleScreenEncounter.enter`): `takeScene`, `createEngine`, then `PaintedStage.stage` builds every figure in parallel (`tryRigFigure` awaits the rig's pieces; `PaintedActor.create` awaits the opening poses and, for the others, up to the 3 s ceiling); the moment a figure exists `prefetchLights` asks for **all of its maps** (40 files in Chapter I) and `loadStreamed` starts **six later poses per figure** (five figures: thirty at once); the battle preload's phase 4 is still downloading the other poses if the pre-battle scene was too short to finish it. Nothing is waited for among those, and all of it shares the line with what is.
- **Why Chapter IV is worse.** Its pre-battle scene is 19.0 s against 35 to 40: it hides about 24 MB of the 94, so the entry begins with the line already behind.
- **How much of the line the entry consumed** (second review, from `first-command-phone10-pad-cand68.json`): `entry-load` ended when **86 percent (Chapter I) and 88 percent (Chapter IV)** of the post-press bytes had crossed the line (74.9 of 87.3 MB and 77.1 of 87.8 MB); at the entry's start 59 and 30 percent had. So the entry consumed about **23 MB (Chapter I) and 51 MB (Chapter IV)** of line against roughly 10 MB it needs by the load diet's own account (the rig 4.7 MB, the backdrop, the ready poses). The cut removes what is asked for *after* `begin`. Chapter I: the gap of about 13 MB is the lighting maps (17.9 MB), so 8 to 10 s is a fair central estimate. Chapter IV: of 41 MB the maps are 13.7, the preload's remainder is now gated, the audio (16.6 MB, 13 s of line) **is not**, and requests already in flight when `begin` fires (`FETCH_WINDOW` 3 poses, about 3 MB) stay. **No CPU lever**: with no network limit the load wait is 4.2 s (Chapter I) and 7.6 s (Chapter IV) against an 8 s budget, and nothing in the branch touches `createEngine`, the backdrop, `measureAlpha`/`cleanMatte` on the main thread or the warm-up.

- **The FFX-2 HUD leak is fixed** (DOM flat from the third board at 1905 to 1910 against 2,337 to 2,822 and rising); it was not the cause of the renderer's and the GPU's climb, which is about the same in the candidate as in live release 67.
- **JS is flat and the DOM is flat, so what grows is native**: decoded images and canvases, textures, decoded audio, driver pools. Chapter I five times is flat (the second visit finds its own set); the two chapters alternating climb for eight visits and then flatten at 800 MB (renderer) and 2.7 GB (GPU). That is the shape of caches filling to their caps with the working set of **two** chapters, not of a per-visit leak.
- **The two caches, from the code.** `PaintedArtCache`: 96 paintings or 900 MB, the intent in its own comment "the chapter being played plus roughly the last two, a retry's worth" (Chapter I has 48 pose files and Chapter IV 43 by the offline model, so both fit under 96). `FigureRelight`: 56 maps, 112 while a figure pins one, evicting **one map per load** (Chapter I has 40 maps, Chapter IV 28). Music (`MusicLoader.evict`, 3 cues), the governor (disposed with the stage), the stage, the actors, the scene (`scene.dispose` frees backdrop, geometry, materials, particles) and the listeners (a scan of 61 global-target listeners and 228 module-scope collections found nothing written and never cleared) are disposed or bounded.
- **The transitions say more than the boards** (second review, renderer private MB at desk50, A B A B A, Chapter I and IV; enter n = board n-1 to the first menu of battle n, leave n = that menu to board n):

| | enter 2 (first visit of IV) | leave 2 | enter 3 (revisit I) | leave 3 | enter 4 (revisit IV) | leave 4 | enter 5 (revisit I) | leave 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r67 | **+107** | +1 | **+92** | -29 | **+94** | -12 | **+88** | -20 |
| cand68 | +14 (a baseline artefact: its board 1 reads 434 MB against r67's 321) | -8 | **+103** | -32 | **+127** | -14 | **+98** | -22 |

  A revisit costs +88 to +127 MB at entry, as much as the first visit of the other chapter in r67, and leaving returns only 8 to 32 MB. The mechanism says a revisit finds its chapter's paintings and maps already decoded, so a cache hit adds about nothing, and Chapter I's 48 pose files are about 150 MB decoded at 1x (754 master headers sampled, 3.04 MB each; desk50 is under the 2,560 px buffer that moves the base tier to 2x): **one** step at the first visit of the second chapter and nothing after. The lane's own control arm (`battle-screen-cycles` with `?memrelease=off`) reads exactly that, 12, 24, 24, 24, 24. The browser reads six steps. Either the revisit paintings are not hits (then dropping at fight end changes nothing a revisit pays), or the +100 MB per entry is something per fight that is in neither cache (diorama, rig pieces, decoded audio, HUD) and is not released on leaving. Neither reading is helped by `dropPaintingsBefore`. The metric is Windows `PrivateMemorySize64`, which follows committed allocator memory and so the high-water mark, and the release leaves the in-fight peak unchanged (a fight holds the chapter before it until it ends, B5c-01). Rows B5a-10 to B5a-12 read exactly these transitions in the browser.
- **What the memory work cannot see without a browser:** the GPU process is a high-water mark (its freed memory returns to the driver's pools slowly), `chrome` decodes images lazily and discards them under pressure, and the "private" numbers are Windows' accounting. The predictions are therefore loose on the GPU (section 4).

## 3. What this does not do (said plainly)

- **B3a (50 MB to the first menu on a phone) stays FAILED.** The bytes are the same in another order; the first menu comes earlier on a line that is busy the whole time, so fewer bytes have crossed it by then (0.8 to 0.95 expected), not 50. The lever on the bytes is the half-size phone derivative of the 1x set (`load-diet.md` section 5; a phone shows a figure at most ~600 device pixels tall and fetches ~1,200): a third of the bytes, a new kind of tier and a picture to approve. **It needs Bailey's yes** (rule 10) and is not built.
- **B2a for the phone in Chapter IV will not reach 8 s by ordering alone** (about a 15 percent chance): the entry consumed 51 MB of line against about 10 MB it needs, the audio is not held, and the CPU floor is 7.6 s. Chapter I may (central 8 s).
- **The desktop's B2a is not touched** (5.9 s and 4.4 s against 5.0 s at 50 Mbit/s: the line is not the limit there, the scene hides 250 MB; it is the entry's work floor, `post-fight-start.md`: 2.1 to 5.2 s bimodal). The brief said without changing the desktop.
- **The boss rig's normal pieces are still awaited at the rig's load** (`RigFigure.load(..., normals)`); the offline model's 17.9 MB of "maps" counts pose maps only, so the rig's lighting pieces are an unknown share of the 16.8 MB G4 saw at the entry. Deferring them is a second change of the same kind (open question 4).
- **Audio is not touched and not held** (rule 13: Bailey's ear): 17.7 MB after the press (16.6 MB in Chapter IV, 13 s of line), of which the SFX sprites (8.2 MB) are not needed by the first command. Holding it behind the figures is ordering, not content, and the likeliest next lever for Chapter IV; B2a-17 to B2a-19 read how much of it crosses the line before the stage mark.
- **The CPU floor has no lever.** The brief named decode and shader compile; with no network limit the load wait is 4.2 s (Chapter I) and 7.6 s (Chapter IV, main thread 60 to 65 percent busy). A Chapter IV load wait under 8 s at 10 Mbit/s needs a CPU change as well as the bytes (rows B2a-16, B2a-20 read the floor).
- **The desktop revisit gets colder.** The release makes the chapter not played last cold on its next visit (about 150 MB to decode again; `cleanMatte` was 0.9 s of main thread for 30 paintings). The preload hides it when the scene is long, not when it is skipped. B5d-01 reads the entry time of revisits, old arm against new (by the API, no prep and no preload: pessimistic).
- **A hole the release does not reach (pre-existing):** figures built after the stage is disposed are never disposed (`PaintedStage.stage()/add()` register a figure after `await PaintedActor.create(...)` with no disposed check), so an `enter()` abandoned during `stage()` leaves figures whose light pins are never unpinned. Not reachable from the player's UI during the entry (the pause cannot open then), reachable by the API; the budgets job leaves only through completed fights and will not see it.
- **Smaller:** `?memrelease=off` still unbinds the eye-candy scene (it is release 68 "in retention" only); `performance.mark` entries are never cleared (7 per fight, immaterial); commit `2f7162cd8`'s message says "61 percent" and "20 s" where this note and the code say 74 percent and two minutes (`36b2082b1` corrected the code).
- **The memory change is a hypothesis, and probably not the whole ramp** (about one in five that B5a-02 lands in -5 to +25 percent). It removes what the code shows is kept for the wrong chapter; whether that is the climb is the first thing the job measures. Said in advance: if the books show the mechanism working (B5a-06, -08, -09) and the counters stay up, the code is right and the next step is the in-fight peak (release the chapter just left on a victory, keep it only after a loss, the only time a retry is likely).
- **A KO in the first seconds of a very slow line** still lies down in its standing painting until revived (the load diet's open item); the ranked queue starts `ko` with the everyday moves (need 1), as the load diet's review fix ordered it.
