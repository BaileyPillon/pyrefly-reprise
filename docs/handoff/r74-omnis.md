## 3. The design, and why it is this one

- **The flag's real box, not a bigger guess.** The solver is given what the browser draws. The harness's pairs are measured on that same box (`getBoundingClientRect` of `.ffx-target__plate`, the skew included).
- **Only once the flag lies on the advisor.** The advisor's placement is held per decision on purpose (a re-solve every frame made the card teleport: `solveAdvisorPlacement`). A flag that never touches the card gives the solver nothing, so the card stands exactly where release 72 put it in the windows and stops that read 0 (1280, 2560, 3440 at TEXT SIZE 100; both 115 windows): the model checks that placement is deep-equal to the no-flag one at every stop of those windows.
- **Held with a margin, replaced only when the flag leaves it.** The flag drifts 5 to 8 grid px over a walk (the figure sways; the dock flips sides). A box that followed every pixel would re-solve the card at each; the held box takes the drift in. In the model the card changes place **at most 5 times in a 29-stop walk** (against 15 to 23 distinct flag places).
- **The solver's own answer.** With the flag as one more solid box the solver returns (a) the card on clear ground (the model: the same compact card, its foot **about 35 grid px higher**, from y 162 to about 127 to 130, and a little shorter: its box is 74 tall against 94), (b) the one-row tip on the top row, or (c) a decline: the card put away and the `N` chip left on clear ground. (c) is the "hidden while the cursor is up" alternative the hold's lift condition names; (a) is "steered against the flag's dock boxes". Neither can paint over the flag by construction (the solver keeps GAP = 6 grid px from every box, the held box is already 4 grid px beyond the flag).
- **No loop.** The flag docks off the panels it was handed at the last key press and the advisor's card among them (stale by design); the advisor reads the flag after it has docked; nothing re-docks the flag from the advisor's new place until the next key press, so each press is one best-response step, not a chase.
- **The dock learns the Enemy Intent line, the Sensor card learns the flag.** Both are the same lane's smaller pieces, both gated, both with the recorded stop that asks for them.
