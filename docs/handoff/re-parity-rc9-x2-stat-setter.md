## 2. How it is built

- **Kernel** (`src/battle/ffx2/kernel/stat-setter.ts`, 264 lines; pure, no DOM, no `three`, no random draw, deterministic): `STAT_ROWS` (the 40 plain stores: offset, width, form, the recompute flag, the helper call), `storedValue` (the five forms), `groupSlots`, `setStat`; the world is an interface of `u8/u16/u32(slot, offset)` reads, so a reader that lacks a field fails loudly (the replay test answers only the fields the fixture holds).
- **Adapter** (`adapt/stat-setter.ts`, 99 lines): the units become the world (the game slot of each unit from `assignSlotIds`; in the battle = not removed and not a fallen monster that left; gone = `hidden`; the applied word's Death bit = the `ko` status; species, the motion byte and the model object read 0). A store reaches the engine through a short list: hit points (`+0x3b4`), the death pattern (`+0x2d8`; the `+0x2da` copy is the same byte), Agility (`+0x39a`). A store to any other offset throws with the offset and the stat id, so a script that starts to use an id the engine does not model is a failing test, not a lost write; a hit-point write that clears the Death bit throws with the name of row XE3; a write to a girl throws (the shipped scripts write monsters only).
- **Death entries** (`engineHooks.ts`): `markDeathEntry` (the request) and `runDeathEntries` (the pass), `AiScript.onDeath` (internal.ts), `ResolveContext.onKo` (called at the end of the knock-out path in `applyHpDelta`, after Auto-Life has had its turn); two calls in `engine-core.ts` (end of `afterAction`, end of `runLogicStep`). Only a script that defines `onDeath` answers: the Core's, the only one.
- **Death patterns** (`adapt/death-patterns.ts`, 66 lines): keyed `table/row` of the monster row the enemy already carries (`ffx2Record`); `deathPatternOf`, `leavesOnDeath`.
- **No contract file changed** (`common/types.ts` is untouched; `Ffx2Unit` and `AiScript` live in the FFX-2 engine's `internal.ts`), so there is no `CONTRACT-CHANGES.md` entry. **400-line rule**: `engine-core.ts` 399, `internal.ts` 396, `engineHooks.ts` 324, `setup.ts` 361; the new files are 66 to 264 lines. `node tools/orphans.mjs`: the three new modules are reached; the only orphans under `src/battle/ffx2` are the same three as before (`kernel/dressphere-recalc.ts`, `kernel/spherechange.ts`, `fixtures.ts`).

## 3. Sources and the proof against the game

- **The vectors.** `vectors_layer1_final.jsonl.gz` of the note's run holds, for every id, the real setter's answer in the harness (the callees logged, not run): the number of slots visited, the changed bytes (the first 24) and the routines asked for (the first 8). The worlds were not kept; they are drawn from a seeded generator, and `D:\Tools\ffx-parity\rc9\x2-stat\regen.py` redraws them in pure Python. **The independent port of the note reproduces the logged answer (count and changed bytes) of all 24,288 vectors of the 44 ids checked, the 42 ported ids, id 198 and id 3, from the redrawn worlds (0 bad)**, so the redrawn worlds are the harness's. `export_fixture.py` cuts them to what the kernel reads and keeps 4,209 vectors in 61 worlds: three group specs (own, a slot number, a species word) x 23 values x one world (four for the ids whose answer depends on the world).
- **The group-mask builder run for real.** Layer 1 drew species words at random, so none of its 1,403 species vectors matched a slot. `export_masks.py` runs the real builder (live `0x6117b0`) in the harness over 24 worlds with matching species words, own with owners 0 to 255, slot numbers and the edge values (3,120 calls, **0 mismatches against the port**, 116 species groups with members, 23 with several) and exports the game's own masks.
- **What the compiled scripts write.** `stat_setter_usage.json`: the 891 `setstat`/`setstat2` calls of the 36 compiled boss packages (worker, function, group spec, id, value), from the note's disassembly census. They use 46 ids: 42 plain or hand stores, id 198 (no case) and three helpers; and three group specs only (`chr_own` 880 times, the slots 15, 16 and 17, and the Head's species word 4386).
- **The request, read.** The knock-out routine's call of the entry runner (live `0x62b6b0`) builds a request record and hands it to a function (`0x729470`) that takes a free record, fills it with the function and a priority and inserts it into a priority-sorted list on the script thread; the entry body is not run there [R: read with the harness's capstone disassembler, not run]. That is why the Core's entry runs after the action in flight has applied all its results, and why an all-enemy move's later hits on the Bulwarks land as before (pinned).
- **What was new beyond the note.** (1) The note's census gives every shipped boss's death pattern; its difference table has no row for them, and the engine had none. (2) **The existence flag** (id 224, `Chr+0x1788`): the packages of the Nodes (m284 to m286), the Bulwarks (m288, m289), the Redoubts (m291, m292), the inert second Shuyin (m293, which also leaves the battle, id 6) and the Experiment's parts (m195) write it as 0 at init. FFX2-G10 10.1 says the battle-end evaluation skips a monster that does not exist; G10 open question 9 asks how the Leg's Nodes and the Experiment's parts leave it. **The two checked notes together answer it** [I from two [R] notes]: they leave because their init clears the flag. The engine already feeds these units as "not present" through `flags.isPart`; a test now pins that the engine's parts are exactly the packages that clear the flag (35 enemies of 33 packages checked, the Nodes, Bulwarks and Redoubts the only parts among them). (3) The game's status process (`kernel/status-timers.ts`, proven) skips a character with 0 hit points: a Bulwark left at 0 gets no Regen payout and its clocks stop until a hit knocks it out. (4) The package of the Fiend Arena Trema (m328) holds two actors that write death patterns 1 and 2; the note's table lists 2; which actor is the monster was not read, so that row is left out and the arena Trema keeps 0 (it is an OFF option).

### 5.3 Why nothing moves on the intended line, and where something does (the causes)

1. **The Core's entry has nothing to act on.** In all 490 Chapter V seeds that reach the Body link and win it, **both Bulwarks are already down** when the Core falls (980 of 980: the intended line kills the arms first and the Core revives them only between turns): the entry stores 0 over 0, no event and no state moves. The 500-seed row of Chapter V is therefore equal to the base seed by seed. The 3 Bulwark knock-outs after the Core's in those seeds are the same 3 in the base.
2. **The Active clock does move, in the seeds where a standing Bulwark meets the entry.** At 1.5 s of human decision time (the goldens' second arm, `driveChapter5(seed, 1500)`), base against branch, event log by event log (the live status payloads inside earlier `status-add` events aside, which keep counting down and so differ at the end of a run): **Chapter V, 300 seeds: 9 differ (seeds 8, 16, 28, 80, 83, 100, 130, 264, 273), all won in both trees, wins 244 > 244, 1,256,758 events > 1,256,713** (3 to 10 events fewer in the branch); **Chapter IV, 200 seeds: 0 differ; Chapter VI, 200 seeds: 0 differ** (wins 187 > 187). In the three seeds that were read in full (8, 16, 28) the Right Bulwark had just been revived by the Core's Full-Life, a two-hit move killed the Core with its first hit and its second hit landed on that Bulwark as before; the entry then left it at 0 HP, where the game's status process skips it (no Regen payout, no clock), and a later hit knocked it out a few events sooner (seed 8: 906 events instead of 916; its Regen payout of 93 is gone). Ablation: with the entry's two calls removed, seed 8 equals the base again. That is the lane's one visible effect, and the reason `ffx2-atb-golden` `CH5_D1500` seed 8 is re-pinned (`8fc4b62f1e0c4fa4` to `1984ed19b218f2c4`, in git at `748038a0a`; cause written in the file's header).
3. **The first version ran the entry inside the knock-out, and moved 3 of 10 golden seeds (3, 6, 8).** Seeds 3 and 6 were an all-enemy move whose later hits on the Bulwarks (3,000 HP, hit for 2,000) now killed them, because the entry had already zeroed them. Reading the entry runner (section 3) shows the game queues the request, so the entry now runs after the action; seeds 3 and 6 are byte-identical to the base again. The number is in the file so a reader sees why the entry is a request.
4. **The death patterns move nothing**: a fallen non-raisable monster was never the target of a revival on any shipped line (the scripts raise only the Bulwarks and the Redoubts, which are pattern 0), and no monster carries Auto-Life.
5. **Row XE3 has no reach today, measured**: in 500 seeds of Chapter VI at the zero-decision-time line and 60 at 1.5 s, the three Act III monsters were knocked out **0 times** while the combo flag was up (163 knock-outs of the trio, 78 combos played, at 1.5 s). ## 6. Limits and decisions made on the way (each is a boundary or a stand-in, not a game number)
