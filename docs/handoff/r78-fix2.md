## 1. The MAJOR: "a Mix writes a different status word in the engine than the game's kernel does" (carried from release 77)

**What the review had:** `parity-ffx-engine-status.test.ts` left the Flooded Ways' nine rows and then the Wendigo's eight rows out of its seeded sample, because with them in the draw reached an engine-against-kernel difference in the status words (perm 512 against 2,048, then 514 against 2,050: bit 0x200 Berserk against 0x800 Threaten). The same file on release 77's own files failed at other seeds (1, 2, 4, 5, 6 at 20,000 situations: Brimstone, Thunderblast, Grothia's summon, Brimstone, Aqua Toxin). Nobody had investigated.

**Traced by running it (rule 3), not by grepping.** I took the filters off and swept the whole catalog 20,000 situations at a time, collecting every disagreement instead of stopping at the first (scratch: `probe/collect.test.ts`; field-by-field diff of the engine's outcome against the oracle's). Only multi-hit actions disagreed (the Mixes and summons, which are the only non-physical, non-reflectable multi-hit commands in the pool), and the generator's fuzzed records (45 percent of the draws) are what reach the odd corners. **Three causes, two in the engine and one in the test's own oracle:**

| # | Cause | Where | Whose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A landed Threaten's decay was written only when the Threaten bit went from clear to set in the hit record.** The kernel returns the monster's live Threaten byte at 70 percent of itself (never under 1) for every landing and the game keeps it. A later hit of the same action lands Threaten again (the step reads the target as the action began, so it is free to), and so does a hit in which Berserk, Confuse or Provoke clears bit 11 before Threaten sets it back: the record's bit never goes clear to set, so the engine left the byte at 70 where the kernels had 49, and the next roll met a larger percent. Situation 3230 of seed 20261009 (a fuzzed Abaddon Flame: Berserk and Threaten at 254, three hits) is exactly this; the last hit's Threaten roll succeeded in the engine and failed in the kernels, hence 0x802 against 0x202. | `src/battle/ffx/adapt/status-apply.ts` | the engine |
| 2 | **A hit nullified by a Nul ticked the target's status but not the hit record.** The Nul check reads and edits the record's four Nul counters. A Nul that a later hit of the same action lands was then compared with the unticked counter: a one-charge Nul spent by hit 2 and landed again by hit 3 was never given back (the engine saw "no change" in the record). The seeded sweep found it as `words.counters` on Thunderblast, Dark Rain, Krysta and Black Ice. | `src/battle/ffx/hit-apply.ts` (`withNulCounters`) | the engine |
| 3 | **The oracle priced a fraction-of-the-maximum hit from the starting maxima** after an earlier hit had turned Double HP or Double MP on (a fuzzed Final Elixir: Double MP then a full restore). The engine reads the target as it stands (the file's own header says the pipeline does), the oracle read the situation's starting maxima: MP 507 against 570. | `tests/unit/helpers/ffxEngineStatus.ts` (`oracleRun`) | the test |

**Not established, stated plainly:** whether the exe applies Double HP / Double MP per hit or at the write-back is not something this pass proved (no new reverse engineering). The oracle now agrees with the engine's stated model ("each hit's statuses are applied at once"). It only matters to a multi-hit action that turns Double HP or MP on and then prices a fraction-of-the-maximum hit; no shipped record is such (the Final Elixir that disagreed was a fuzzed one).

**Fixed, filters off.** `parity-ffx-engine-status.test.ts` samples the whole catalog again (`FLOODED_ROWS` and `WENDIGO_ABILITIES` imports and both filters gone; the comment says what the difference was). **Before the fix:** the default 5,000 situations failed at 3230 with the Wendigo rows in. **After:** 21 seeds (20261009 and 1 to 20) of 20,000 situations over the whole catalog, 420,000 situations, **0 disagreements** (the collecting probe prints `SUMMARY seed N bad 0` for each). Two new tests keep it from coming back: (a) `parity-ffx-status-chain.test.ts` (7 tests) pins the two engine rules by hand with the smallest action that shows each (Threaten 100 to 70 to 49 to 34 over three hits; a one-charge Nul-Tide landed, spent and landed again); **3 of the 7 fail on the old engine and pass on the new** (I put the old conditions back to check, then restored the fix); (b) the status file gains one test that sweeps seven seeds (20261009, 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8) of 20,000 situations each, 140,000 draws, in about 33 s.

**Inert for every shipped chapter (proof, not assumption).** A scan of the 585 recorded abilities (`scan-records.mjs` in the scratch folder): Threaten (status 11) is in `threaten`, `heavenly-strike`, `sin-fin-negation`, `sin-fin-negation-far` and `sin-core-negation`, **all single-hit**, and at a first hit the old and new conditions are the same (a landed Threaten needs the live bit clear, and at an action's first hit the record equals the live words). A Nul status is in 26 records (three of them the Sin negations, which carry Threaten too), **only `dark6-oblivion` is multi-hit (16 hits) and its element is none**, which no Nul can turn aside, so a nullified hit followed by a landing cannot happen. The Wendigo's rows carry neither. **The replay of every FFX and FFX-2 chapter** (all listed and every earlier hidden chapter, the shipped `intended` strategy and `attack`, 25 seeds, whole chains) on this tree against the review's baseline of `r78-int` (`ffx-diff.mjs`, `ffx-int.txt`): **1,725 event-log hash lines, byte-identical** (the Wendigo and the Ifrit chapters included).
