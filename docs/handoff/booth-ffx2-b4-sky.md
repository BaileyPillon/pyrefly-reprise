## 4. The three lighter options (`?skygrade=a|b|c`; nothing plays by default)

The word reaches Chapter V and Chapter XI only, only while the treatment reaches them (`?treated=pack` and an address that chose a look by hand answer release 68), and plays the held `hd2d` look and `arena` grade with one change on the room's pro table (the table that failed the read; the shipped rows are never written: a test reads them back after every option). `__pyrefly.skyGrade('a'|'b'|'c'|null)` switches live on a page that opened with the word (the LUT key carries it); `skyGrade(null)` plays the r70 grade unpatched on the same page and the same frame: the **control arm**. `?pro=off` with the word reads the bold look alone (the brightest row of the r70 read).

| option | idea | change on the room's pro table | what it does to the picture (predicted) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a** exposure cap | one lever | exposure capped at 1.0 (the rows are 1.45 and 1.5) | the whole sky falls: the pro colour transform is steep (a gamma of 0.26, the exponent 3.85), so 1.0 against 1.45 is about a quarter of the light at the same input; the burst's core is what is left bright |
| **b** highlight roll-off | one lever | a soft knee on the colour transform (`ProColour.shoulder` 0.5, tanh above it; the rows have none) and exposure capped at 1.2 | no clip at all up to four times the live sky: the burst keeps its gradation, the plate reads as a moody dusk |
| **c** dimmer bold row | one lever | the added-light terms come down: beams 2.0 to 0.8, stage light 1.7 to 1.2, bloom strength 0.85 to 0.45 and threshold 0.66 to 0.8, figure key 1.5 to 1.3, the cone 0.7 of itself, exposure capped at 1.2 | the burst throws less light into its own sky; the grade still clips where the sky's own pixels are bright |

**The engine's own LUT, offline** (the real `lutBytes` through the patched room; the probe is the live sky at the 90th percentile of luma in the band above the party, 0.98 / 0.78 / 0.98 raw, read off the r70 still; "clip at xN" is the multiple of the live sky at which the LUT first puts the probe over 240; pinned in `booth-ffx2-b4-sky-grade.test.ts`):

| | clip at (V, XI) | probe Y at x1.25 (V, XI) | at x1.5 (V, XI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| r70 grade (the control) | x1.29, x1.28 | 218, 225 | 255, 255 |
| a | x2.08, x2.11 | 52, 54 | 114, 115 |
| b | no clip to x4 | 85, 86 | 137, 136 |
| c | x1.60, x1.62 | 123, 123 | 212, 203 |

The unclipped pixels of the r70 still sit at 1.16 to 2.24 times the live sky (the median 1.78): the engine adds that much light before the grade (bloom, the beams, the stage light), which is why the r70 grade, clipping at x1.3, whites out 64 percent of the band. **An offline model of the whole frame was tried and does not reproduce the browser** (the real LUT applied to the live still with a radial additive light fitted to the r70 still: rms 42.6 levels on the band, 92 percent of the band predicted over 240 against 64.2 measured), so the options carry **no predicted band numbers beyond the ranges below**; the browser job decides, and the stills are for Bailey's eye first.

**Predictions (rule 15; the sky band, Chapter V's link 1 first menu at 2560 x 1440; r70 control 232.5 mean and 64.2 percent over 240, release 69 179.4 and 0.2), as revised after the review (the first draft said a 110 to 190 and 5 to 30, b 90 to 150 and 0 to 2, c 140 to 215 and 10 to 50; the review read them against the ladder above and found b and c low):** the unclipped pixels of the r70 still sit at 1.16 to 2.24 times the live sky with a median of 1.78, and the clipped 64 percent sit higher still, so the band's pre-grade light is mostly at x1.5 to x2.5 of the live sky. The lilac probe reads, at x1.5 / x1.78 / x2: **a** 113 / about 178 / 229; **b** 136 / about 175 / 202; **c** 208 / clipped from x1.61 / 255. From that: **a**: band mean 110 to 200, over 240 5 to 45 percent (the core only, if the gain tail is short); **b**: band mean 120 to 200, over 240 **0 to 1 percent, by construction** (b's LUT never puts any input above luma 210 in Chapter V or 212 in Chapter XI, neutral white at x3 included, so "under 3 percent over 240" is met whatever b looks like: **the number cannot be the filter for b, and a flat grey-lilac plate would pass it; Bailey's eye on the stills is**); **c**: band mean 150 to 230, over 240 20 to 60 percent (it clips at x1.6 against the r70 grade's x1.3, so it is the one most likely to fail 3 percent; the beams may matter more than the table says). These are readings of a model that did not reproduce the browser, not measurements. If none clears: combine (`skyGrade('b')` with `proTune('farplane', { colour: { exposure: ... } })` from the console) before asking Bailey, or leave the look held; that is a look decision (hard rule 9), picked from stills.

**Label a still by `screen`, not by the word.** `__pyrefly.skyGrade()` returns `{ word, active, armed, screen, chapters, options }`: `armed` is whether the look rig and the pro arena are up (the only way the patched table reaches the screen), `screen` is the picture a still taken now shows (`release69`, `r70`, `a`, `b`, `c`, or null outside Chapters V and XI). On a page opened **without** the word the battle sets up with the look held, so `skyGrade('b')` mid-battle changes `active` and leaves `screen` at `release69`: the review reproduced a still that would have been labelled b and was release 69's. To film an option, open the page with `?skygrade=` (the control arm is `skyGrade(null)` on that page, `screen` `r70`). The setter does not arm the rig mid-battle on purpose: `lookState.armed` is also what makes the figures bind their relight maps (`LookState.ts`), and that happens as the battle sets up, so a rig armed late would be a third picture nobody has checked, neither the option nor the control.
