## 1. The finding and the brief

`r73-sinface` traced Chapter XVIII's 17 one-frame hops to the house strike played at the `fast` playback speed (0.32): `resolveStrike` multiplies every phase by it, the travel lands in about 5 frames, the way home in 8, and the figure steps 0.461 and 0.251 of its reach in one frame (0.153 and 0.081 at normal). Today's lunge uses a fixed `LUNGE_MS`, ignores the speed and has no hops. The brief: keep a smooth travel at every battle speed by flooring the travel so its largest one-frame step, as a share of the figure's drawn height, never exceeds what the same strike takes at normal speed; the rest of the move still speeds up; Chapters I and IV at normal stay byte for byte; prove it with the real actor and `detectJerks`; then decide the chapters.

**What the measurement added to the brief: the way home needs the same floor.** With the travel alone floored, the real actor under the real detector still reads an isolated step on the way home at fast, on the frame of the swap: 113 px (280 px drawn, reach 3), 166 (reach 4.4), 173 and 253 (420 px), 257 and 376 (600 px), ratio 5.6 to 6.2 against the neighbouring steps (the lunge's own jerks are 27 to 61 px, ratio 4.4). Those are the seven `attack>follow` hops of the review (113.6 to 256.4 px). So the floor covers the two phases where the figure crosses the stage, the **travel** and the **return**, and nothing else.

## 2. The fix (code map)

- `src/engine/motion/StrikeCurve.ts`: `crossingFloors(profile, heftFactors)` answers the travel and the return at their normal-speed length (paced ms: `profile ms x heft factor / defaultPaceFactor`); `resolveStrike` takes the larger of the speed-scaled phase and that floor. **Applied only when `timeScale < 1`, only to a house profile** (`profile.real` absent), and not when `StrikeOptions.floorCrossings === false` (the control arm; nothing in the game sets it). At `timeScale` 1 or more the code path is the old one, so every shape is the same object-for-object (a test compares them with `toEqual`; an earlier version, `Math.max` at every speed, differed from release 72 by one ULP, 5e-14 ms, and was dropped for this exactness). 371 -> 389 lines.
- `src/engine/motion/StrikePlan.ts` (`floorCrossings?: false`) and `ActorCurves.ts` (hands it to `resolveStrike`): the control arm, used by `r73-sinface-trace.test.ts` to keep release 72's strike as the cause record.
- **The blow must still land on the strike.** The floor makes the contact later at fast: wind-up x 0.32 + the whole travel = 298 ms on screen (the FFX profile at the default pace), not 128 (400 at normal). Three consumers were written for "(wind-up + travel) x speed" and are changed with it (`strikeLagMs`, ms at speed 1 and pace 1, the unit they are written in: the travel's normal length times (1/speed - 1), 443 ms at fast):
  - `ContactBeat.armContact` (the hold cap) and `ContactBeat.meetContact` (a hit's wait for the apex, 400 ms at speed 1): both grow by the lag at a speed under 1, so a hit never gives up on the strike 170 ms before it lands (VP-1001-06; without it the blow would play on air in the middle of the travel). Zero at normal speed, in `skip` (the presenter's sleep is zero there anyway), with the curves off and for a real strike.
  - `BattlePresenterSfx` (`withParity` and the sound pass's `attackBeat`): the swing sound's `beatMs` is `strikeBeatNow(heft, real, playbackScale)`, which adds the lag; the cue's peak stays on the blow.
  - `StrikeBeat.playbackScaleOf(ctx)` reads the scale the presenter's waits use.
- **What it never touches**: a real profile (`?curves=real`, `real-snappy`: Chapter I and Chapter IV play these for their party figures, and so does every treated chapter that does not hold `real`) at any speed (a test compares every `real` and `snappy` attack row of both tables at 1, 0.32 and 0.02 with and without the flag: more than 40 shapes); `FlipFit` and `ReturnPace` (they answer undefined for a house strike); today's lunge (`?curves=today`); the wind-up, the hold, the settle and the snap.
- Tests move with it: `strike-trace-fixture.ts` is new (the harness of `r73-sinface-trace.test.ts` moved out and shared; `play` gains the `floorCrossings` flag and a `contact` mark), `r73-sinface-trace.test.ts` plays release 72's strike (`floorCrossings: false`) and reads exactly what it read (16 tests, unchanged numbers).
