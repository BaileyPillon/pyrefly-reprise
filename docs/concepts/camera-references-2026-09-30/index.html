Clair Obscur and Persona Cameras

How Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, Persona 5 and Persona 5 Royal point the camera at each moment of a battle. Every timestamp below was checked against a frame of an official video; click one to see the moment in the real game.

Where the camera idea comes from

  1. Persona 5 · 2016

    The battle camera Broche names.

  2. Persona 5 Royal · 2019

    The same battle camera, plus new Showtime scenes.

  3. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 · 2025

    Names Persona 5 as its model.

Sandfall credits Final Fantasy X for the party, the pacing and the story. No source ties Clair Obscur’s camera to FFX.

What both games do

  • While you choose, the camera holds a composed shot of the acting hero in the left third facing the enemy, with the menu attached to the hero instead of a separate panel.
  • Every action cuts to a new shot.
  • Big moments become their own set pieces: short authored films in Clair Obscur, and in Persona mostly flat 2D graphic sequences (cut-ins, comic panels, silhouettes on red), plus Royal’s Showtime scenes.

Where they differ

  • Clair Obscur’s camera moves inside its shots (the lens zooms, the frame shakes, time slows) and it also cues incoming attacks so you can parry.The cue idea is one outlet’s report of a developer talk; the game’s own Camera Movement option text agrees that some attacks get harder to read with it off.
  • Persona’s command shot never moves; only the menu ring animates. Its flair comes from graphic design: typography, cut-ins and panels.Measured on frames: the background did not shift by one pixel across four seconds of command select.

Clair Obscur · 2025

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33

The battle camera is a director rather than a viewpoint: every stage of a turn (choose, pick a target, act, defend) cuts to its own authored shot, with the menu hung in the scene beside the hero, so a turn-based fight plays like an edited film sequence.

01

Battle start (field to arena)

Shock ring, petal burst, wide arena reveal

Camera
A low, tight view of the impact, a petal-burst wipe, then a wide arena shot from the side (from behind and above the party for a boss). The heroes are small in the lower frame and a blur whip hands over to the first command shot.
Frame
Heroes low in frame, enemy across the arena (side-on) or at the top of frame (boss version). The battle HUD fades in during the reveal: enemy name bar first, party HP tiles a beat later, turn-order strip last. No menu until the first actor is up. A ‘First Strike!’ banner shows when the player opened the fight.

The petal burst repeats the game’s Gommage motif, so the swap from field camera to arena camera reads as a designed beat and hides the change of shot. The still wide gives the player the formation before menus arrive. A reviewer singles out the way each hero swishes into battle.

Battle Wheel over the shoulder

Behind and slightly beside the acting hero, who stands in the left third and fills half to two thirds of the frame height, about 2 to 3 m away. The lens is normal and the camera barely drifts; a whip under half a second brings you into the shot.

Attack and skill execution

Each skill is a short film

Camera
Each skill plays its own run of two to five shots: the caster low and full-body, a close-up with the timing prompt, a whip to the enemy for the hit, then a wide pull-back. Expect cuts, blur, shake, slow motion on the impact and some tilted frames.
Frame
The caster fills the frame with a diamond ‘A’ prompt and a ring that reads PERFECT when timed well; large in-scene damage numbers pop at each hit (24 up to 12,437 seen); a ‘Status Effects’ tag sits top-right during casts.
Why it reads as premium
Dev intent: each skill is authored in a sequence editor as a small cinematic with camera position, rotation, a zooming lens, shakes and slow motion, all for a feeling of intensity. A reviewer credits slow motion and particle flourishes with theatrical drama.

Persona 5 · 2016

Persona 5

A near-static, low, wide, rear-side poster shot of the acting character (left) facing the enemy (right), which hosts the whole menu as a fan of labels pinned to the character, and which every command interrupts with a hard-cut, comic-timed cutaway or a flat 2D graphic set piece, so each turn plays like edited anime and manga panels rather than a tracking camera.

Persona’s camera grammar, in six lines

  • Two camera modes only: a stable hero shot that holds indefinitely while the player thinks, and short authored cutaways (about 0.3 to 5 s) on every commit.
  • While a menu is open the camera does not move; the UI animates (ring fans out from the character’s hand, list slides, reticle hops). Background shift over 4 to 19 s of command select measured 0 px.
  • Every cut lands under a graphic beat: ink wipe, shard wipe, typographic burst (WEAK, 1 MORE, HOLD UP!, BATON PASS!), comic panel. The graphic hides the cut and adds the sound-effect feel.
  • The camera is always on the action’s owner or its victim, never on a neutral master; the party HP cards stay pinned bottom-right through 3D cutaways and disappear only in the 2D sequences and Showtime.
  • The climaxes leave the 3D scene: All-Out Attack becomes a flat red plane of silhouettes, Showtime becomes a purpose-built cutscene stage.
  • Persona 5’s director describes the intended feel as turn-based battle built like a cutscene, using the cut-and-paste method of anime and manga; the Clair Obscur creative director describes the same effect as the camera shifting dramatically with each action, like watching a movie. 02

Command select (idle, your turn)

Rear-side poster shot with the menu ring pinned to the character

Same in Royal
Camera
Behind the acting character, about 50 degrees off the line to the enemy, with the character in the left third and the enemy right of centre. Hip height, tilted slightly up, wide lens, and no camera movement at all: only the menu ring animates.
Frame
Character in the left third. A fan of angled black label plates with red slashes (GUN, PERSONA, ITEM, SWORD, GUARD, plus ORDER when the party has more than one member) is anchored at the character’s hand and hip and points at the enemy. Red target marker with a heart icon on the enemy. Enemy name-and-level plate top-left; navigator speech bubble with portrait top-right; one to four slanted HP/SP cards bottom-right (a solo fight shows one); five button hints bottom-left (Analyze, Target, turn-order or status check, Rush, Assist) in the same order with the same icons in the base and Royal frames (the wording of the third hint differs between the English base capture and the Japanese Royal frames; localisation or Royal change not established).
Why it reads as premium
The composition doubles as the menu: hero left, enemy right, UI in the space between, so the screen reads as a poster and the eye travels from hero to enemy. Because the shot is static, the ring, not the camera, does all the animating.
