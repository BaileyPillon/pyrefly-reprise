Recommended pick (an agent's look; you pick)

D, all three together

  • It is the only option that makes every moment richer: A lights the room, B makes it deep and alive between turns, and C makes the blows and payoffs land. They touch different moments, so they add up instead of competing.
  • At thumbnail size the ON frame is unmistakably richer than today in all four chapters, at rest and in combat.
  • The paintings stay the star: nothing repaints them, the combination damps the stacked glows, and the HUD is untouched DOM above the canvas.
  • It costs nothing measurable here: desktop and phone hold 60 fps with the phone tier, the same as today.
  • Every piece stays separately strikeable (?fxsub=-sway, -hitstop, -splash, -streaks), so you can keep D and drop any re-offer you still do not want.

If D feels busy in play, the next pick is A and B together (?fx=a,b): the lit, living room without C's combat layer, the calmest of the rich options. C alone changes nothing at rest, so it cannot carry the frame on its own.

Judge's note (an independent agent that built none of the four; full report in JUDGE.md). Scores out of 10: beauty A 6, B 7, C 6, D 9; mean over all seven criteria A 7.7, B 7.7, C 7.3, D 7.3. D is the right direction and the only one that reads as "maximum eye candy" at a glance, but not as tuned today. Mega Flare still blooms a white disc over Bahamut. Gagazet's painted moon turns into a sun. Steam and haze bury Bevelle's backdrop at rest. Hit rings hide the fighter they hit. Phone spells clip to blobs. All of these are dial fixes to make before you see D in play. The re-offers (sway, hit-stop, splash, Aerospark's lance) each need their own yes. Checked on a production build by real keys: ?fx=d runs in Chapters I and XVI at 1600x900 and 390x844 with no errors and holds the claimed 60 fps. Without ?fx, the build behaves like main. Fallback to show next: B + C, not yet captured.

A

Light and lens

Golden-Hour Cinema

?fx=a

  1. A cinema post chain over the paintings: each room's own lights (moon, braziers, skylights, work lamps) throw slow beams and light the air round them.
  2. Selective bloom (lights and spells glow, paint and costumes never do), a colour look built from each painting's own palette, grain, vignette, a key-matched rim on the figures.
  3. Big spells, Overdrives and Specials fire a lens flare and make the room's light swell and settle.

FFX Chapters I and VII

Golden Hour: shadows lean to the painting's sky, only the brightest tenth leans gold; soft wide bloom; long horizontal anamorphic streaks; slow beams for the calm of CTB; a gold anamorphic flare over 600 ms.

FFX-2 Chapters IV and XVI

Pink Hour: violet shadows and pink highlights; tighter, stronger bloom; four-point star glints on every lamp; beams 1.6x faster for the ATB pace; a pink spark4 star flare with hex ghosts over 450 ms.

B

Depth and motion

Living Paintings

?fx=b

  1. Each approved painting is cut at run time into 3 or 4 depth plates (from depth maps made on the CPU); on the idle rig the camera arcs slowly and the painted world slides behind the fighters at its own depths.
  2. The room's own air and light come alive: weather between the plates, lamps that glow and flicker, cold light crawling through ice.
  3. Figures belong to the room: soft cast shadows from the key light, Macalania's ice floor mirrors everyone, and a gentle sway (a re-offer, strikeable).

FFX Chapters I and VII

Gagazet: blizzard veils between the ridges, snow at three depths, the moon halo breathing, long blue moon shadows. Macalania: the reflecting ice floor, warm brazier halos, crystals twinkling. No pyreflies added (canon).

FFX-2 Chapters IV and XVI

Bevelle: steam rising from the vents and banked between the gantries, rust lamps, cyan shimmer; no motes ("steam, not motes"). Djose: amber work lamp, blue arcs, dust, the floor projected so everyone stays planted. Drift runs 0.8x faster than FFX.

C

Impact and payoff

Spectacle Combat

?fx=c

  1. Blows land hard: velocity-streak sparks, a double shock ring, a real hit-stop that freezes the field (never the engine or the HUD), a trauma-model camera shake.
  2. A two-frame ink-and-paper impact frame on heavy blows, and 3D spell layers on top of the approved spells: flame columns, ice spikes, crawling lightning, nova rings.
  3. Overdrives and Specials get a splash cut-in on an Ink & Gold slab, then a payoff: gold ribbons, Mega Flare's shock shell, Ixion's lance. At rest C adds nothing on purpose.

FFX Chapters I and VII

Gold and ivory with navy ink; 85 ms hit-stop on heavy blows; a calmer shake; an ivory splash slab with gold rules; gold ribbons for Overdrives; a 2.6 s victory arc; warm pyreflies on a send-off.

FFX-2 Chapters IV and XVI

Pink and magenta with plum ink; hit-stop only on crits (ATB pace); a sharper shake; a pink slab with four-point stars; Mega Flare shell and Aerospark lance; Chapter IV holds with no celebration (canon).

D

A + B + C, balanced · recommended

All Three Together

?fx=d

  1. A's light, B's depth and weather and C's combat payoff at once, behind one switch (?fx=d).
  2. Where two options light the same thing, the combination is damped so they stop stacking: A's bloom 0.75x, haze 0.5x and beams 0.8x over B's lamps (0.4x); A's flare 0.7x over C's payoff. Each option alone is unchanged.
  3. C's hit-stop now also holds B's drift and weather, so a freeze reads as one held frame.

FFX Chapters I and VII

Gagazet: moon rays through blizzard veils and three depths of snow, long moon shadows. Macalania: gold brazier streaks and skylight beams mirrored in the ice floor, with ice spikes and gold ribbons reflected under the fighters. No pink, no four-point stars, no added pyreflies.

FFX-2 Chapters IV and XVI

Bevelle: the pink-hour grade and lamp stars through banked steam; Mega Flare's shell with the pink star flare. Djose: the amber lamp in dusty air, arcs and the cold beam, crawling lightning. No gold motes, and Chapter IV still holds at the end.
