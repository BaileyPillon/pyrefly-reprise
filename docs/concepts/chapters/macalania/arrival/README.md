# Anima's arrival — three stagings of the money shot (FFX) — end-state options

**Nothing here is built.** No file under `src/` was touched. These are hard-rule-9
option frames for the fourth pick in `docs/plans/chapter-macalania-review.md` §6.4:
*"Anima's arrival, 3 faked screenshots of the finished moment plus a few lines on
how it plays — this is the money shot and the one thing a still cannot fully answer."*

**Game case (rule 14): FFX only.** Macalania Temple is an FFX chapter and the beat
is a summon inside an FFX CTB battle. *Absence test:* no stage carries `ig--ffx2`,
no ATB bar, no dressphere row, no pink accent — a pick applies here and to no FFX-2
chapter.

## The question Bailey answers

> **Which staging should the arrival be built as — what does the single continuous
> camera move lead with: the creature, the rule change, or the threat?**

`sheet.png` puts the three side by side; the frames are `a.png`, `b.png`, `c.png`
at 1600x900. The `.html` beside each PNG is what rendered it (kit:
`docs/concepts/polish/_kit/`, plates: approved Macalania backdrop A, Seymour B,
Guado Guardian A, and the approved Anima painting).

All three contain the same beats — she arrives from below still bound; every living
Guardian dies on the summon; Seymour stays on the field and stops being targetable;
one continuous move, no cut; an original seal mark, never the canon glyph. They
differ in what the move **leads with**.

## A — from below the ice (leads with the creature)

1. The camera drops to floor height behind the party; the mirror floor fills the
   bottom third and the party are back-lit silhouettes at the edges.
2. Violet light cracks the ice from underneath before anything is visible, and both
   Guardians' CTB rows strike through in the same second — the summon killed them.
3. Chains come up through the floor first and go taut, the horns follow, and the
   single red eye clears the ice last.
4. The camera rises with her in one move and never cuts, so her size is learned by
   how long the rise takes.
5. Control returns with the camera still settling — the fight resumes under her.

## B — Seymour steps back (leads with the rule change)

1. A two-shot held throughout: Seymour left, Anima rising right, both in frame.
2. He steps back out of the light and his reticle dies in place — grey corners, no
   name plate, "Cannot be targeted".
3. The Guardians go out between them, pyreflies lifting off the floor, so the cost
   of the summon is paid on screen and not in the log.
4. Anima's reticle lights gold as his goes out: the target list changes in front of
   you rather than being discovered next turn.
5. He stays visible and idle through act two, which makes his return at her
   dismissal read as a return and not a spawn.

## C — the first Pain (leads with the threat)

1. The rise finishes centre-frame and the push-in continues until she fills it, the
   fayth picture at her neck catching the only warm light in the room.
2. Our own seal mark — concentric rings and eight spokes, not the canon glyph —
   draws itself beneath her as she settles.
3. Her CTB tile lights as the current actor at once: the arrival buys no free turn.
4. The charge band names the attack before it lands — *"Pain: one of you. It will
   not miss."* — and the targeted party slab is outlined in blood red.
5. The KO lands and the frame does not flinch; act two teaches itself in three seconds.

## Recommendation

**A as the staging, with B's two beats folded into its tail:** the rise leads,
because that is the money shot §9.4 asks for, and as the camera settles Seymour
steps back and his reticle dies in the same continuous move, before Anima's first
turn. **C** is the weakest entrance and the strongest shot immediately after one.
(A recommendation, not a decision; rule 10.)

## What these frames are honest about

- All three composite the **approved Anima painting**. §9.4 note 1 says Seymour's
  Anima is a notably **shorter** creature than Yuna's; nothing here shows two sizes,
  so whichever staging is picked must record on its tile whether the shipped
  painting is being read as the short one — a knowing departure, not a silent one.
- The seal in C is an original mark drawn in CSS for this mockup. The real mark is
  a NEW asset (plan §6.1) and is exactly what a still cannot settle.
- Chains, ice cracks and pyreflies are CSS over the approved backdrop A plate; the
  engine would do them as particles and painted layers.
- Frame B draws Seymour untargetable, which is **decision C-2** — the plan's own
  highest-consequence assumption, not a sourced fact.
- Numbers on screen are illustrative staging, not sourced encounter data.
- §9.4's demand is a **continuous camera move**. A still cannot prove it. If Bailey
  cannot choose from these, the next rung is a short motion clip of the winning
  staging, not another still round.
