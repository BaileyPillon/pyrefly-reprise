### 1.2 The driver's inferences (logged, his to overturn)

1. **The girl is visible in her Special** for Yuna and Rikku, as in the game (card A drew the bloom alone). Yuna stands inside the bloom, Rikku rides on the machine's back; **Paine is not readable at battle distance** (GR L10, L81), so for her the painting carrying her small is our addition (D-6). 2. **The portrait on the Special Dress Up stays exactly as built** (his earlier pick, "only on big moves"). Section 2.5 says where it fires.
3. **The sprites are image to image over renders of the real models** (driver pick 220m), not hand-posed and not generated from nothing.

### 1.3 The two readings

**Reading G (the game; the plan).** Each Special is one connected figure, painted once as a whole. Its two named parts are regions of it, cut as three registered layer masks from the split reference renders at the same camera, so each part stays addressable (its plate, gauge, menu, HP and target) while the figure never comes apart. On stage the figure is one painted actor at the main girl's own slot (3.1); the two parts are figure-less anchored parts of that actor.

**Reading T (card A as first drawn; costed only).** The same three layers stand apart as three bodies in the party's places. Each part would be a painted body with its own art, its own place and its own pose set.

### 1.4 What is out

Option B's orbiting pods and its one wide plate; option C's held tableau and girl-sized spirits (reading G has no orbiting parts and keeps three plates; the swords that orbit Paine's figure are the game's own `[frame]`, said below); any new gameplay (no new command or stat, and no new target rule except the cursor order of D-11, which is `[ours]`; under option (b) today's on-screen rule would step right part, main, left part, because it sorts by projected x); any invented number (a time or a size below is a frame reading, a measurement, an existing constant, or marked `[ours]`); the three-state Spherechanges Config row; new sounds (the existing `special-stinger` and cues only; audio stays with the driver's separate track); the girl's ordinary dress changes (untouched); the finishers' cut-in art (the portrait hook already rides them). ### 3.2b What the anchored-part machinery gives, and the five additions S1 needs (from the code-fit check; each read in the continuity tree, read-only)

**It gives:** a figure-less actor (`anchoredActorOptions`: no figure, no shadow) re-placed every frame from the parent's live plane (`placeAnchored`, so a hop or lunge carries it), the cursor's box and aim points (`anchoredQuad`, `anchoredAim`), the numbers' screen anchor through `stage.project(id)`, and the turn ring and selection halo of the stub.

**It does not give, and S1 adds (all additive; every existing anchor, which is a scene's table row for an enemy-side part, behaves exactly as today):**

1. **A source of anchors that is not a scene table.** `figureSpec` reads the anchor by combatant id from `env.slots.partAnchors`, a table a scene publishes; no scene has rows for party parts, and S1 edits no scene. The form's per-pose anchors (from the generated `specialParts.ts`) are merged into the figure environment by the tracker's staging helper (`SpecialStaging.ts`), so `anchorFor` finds them. No scene table is touched.
2. **A parent id the engine does not carry.** The stage reads the parent from `c.flags.partOf`; the engine's pods are built with `flags: {}` (`special/enter.ts`, `buildPod`), so `parentPose()` is undefined and `placeAnchored` returns without placing, which leaves the stub on the clamped spot (the G8 failure again). The tracker supplies the parent (the main's id) to `add` as one optional argument; no engine file changes.
3. **An anchor that follows the pose.** Today `staged.anchor` is fixed at `add` and read through the parent's current plane, and nothing fires on a pose change. A Special's parts swing across the figure between poses (Appendix A: Full Throttle's right wing moves from x 0.28 to 0.72 in its attack), so the lookup is **per painting shown, not per pose name** (a painting can be the mirrored or an alternate one), read every frame (`anchorNow`), and `PartRings` gets a `setAnchor`.
4. **A ring policy that is not the enemy's.** `PartRings.add` draws a persistent violet "fiend" ring (`PART_RING_COLOUR`) for every `onParent` anchor (`ring` is mandatory on the type) and fades it only on a KO. That contradicts section 3.8 ("no enemy chrome") and question 2 (a). S1 adds an optional `ring: { hidden?: boolean; mark?: 'target' | 'acting'; colour?: number }` to `OnParentAnchor`, default unchanged: a form part is `hidden` under question 2 (a), and under (b) or (c) draws a mark only while it is targeted or acting, in the party's gold (`0xf0cf92`) and never violet.
5. **Passes that must leave a stub alone, and the stage's own list.** `applyFormation` takes enemies only and skips anchored actors, so it is safe; the relax pass, the key-feature boxes and `stage(state)` are the risks. `stage(state)` builds its actor set from `state.activeIds`, which with a form up names the two pods, so a re-stage mid-form would create them as ordinary party actors at the clamped spot; the staging helper routes the pods through the anchored path there too (R-11's keep-set), and a test proves no pass moves a stub (`relaxFormation`, the key-feature boxes) and that a re-stage with a form up leaves exactly one painted actor and two stubs.

**Budget.** The first version said the Stage file grows by 6 lines. It is about **65 lines across four files**, the one table section 7 also quotes: `StageFigureRequest.ts` +10, `StageAnchors.ts` +15, `PartAnchors.ts` +20 and `BattlePresenterStage.ts` +20 (call-ins only, and it stays over its cap by that much because it is already 1,083 lines: +12 for these five additions, the rest for the `actor()` redirect of 3.7 and the `setArt` height hook of 3.1), with the logic itself in `SpecialStaging.ts`. ### 3.3 How the three layers register, and which region is which part

### 3.7 How a part's actions, hits and statuses reach the figure (the form actor handle; code-fit check C-3, reconciled in 14.2)

Under S1 a part has a stub, not a body. The presenter reacts to an event by looking up the target's actor, `stage.actor(id)`, and today that lookup for a part id returns the stub, so a flash, a hurt, a petrify or a pose change would land on nothing, and a party-side KO would set the stub's pose and lie it down. **The first version had no mechanism for this**, and its first amendment counted 28 lookup sites (the sites in the seven `BattlePresenter*` files: nine in `...Events.ts`, ten in `...Beats.ts`, four in `...Arrivals.ts`, one each in `...Departures`, `...Recall`, `...Returns`, two in `...BossFx`) and put one guarded call at each of about 14. **A grep of `stage.actor(` under `src/engine` finds 92 sites in 28 files**: the other 64 are the camera and motion modules (`procam/ProPlays.ts` 13, `procam/ProDirector.ts` 6, `KeyPoses.ts` 6, `fx/c/SpectacleFx.ts` 5, `motion/StrikeReach.ts` 4, `ShotFit.ts` 4 and so on), which also receive a part id when the camera frames a target or a strike reaches one, and which no call-in in the presenter files could guard. **So the redirect is one place: `PaintedStage.actor(partId)` returns a `FormActorHandle`** for a form's part id and the unchanged actor for every other id (flag off: the very object it returned). **Its type stays `PaintedActor`**, because the method returns the concrete class (`BattlePresenterStage.ts`, `actor(id): PaintedActor | undefined`) and three typed ports depend on it (`fx/c/SpectacleFx.ts:195` reads `a.visible`, which the 28-member `ActorHandle` port does not have; `SpectacleTypes.ts:9` and `SpellMoments.ts:26,85` type `stage.actor(id)` as `PaintedActor`). So the handle is a **`Proxy` over the figure's own `PaintedActor`** (the class has no `#private` fields): it overrides only the members below and forwards everything else (`visible`, `contentQuad` and the rest) to the figure, with the figure's own members bound, and no port type changes. **The stage's own internals do not use `actor()`**: `placeAnchored`, `anchoredQuad`, `rect`, `project`, `PartRings` and the aim points read the stub through `this.actors.get(id)?.actor`; the one internal reader that does go through `actor()`, the target highlight's `actor: (id) => this.actor(id)` (in the stage's constructor), is pointed at the stub so the highlight stays on the stub (one of the +20 lines of the stage, section 7). The handle has **two modes, keyed on Q-2**, and one table of members:

- **forwards to the figure** (both modes): `setPose`, `flash`, `shake`, `recoil`, `hitReact`, `squash`, `hop`, `liftTo` and `liftRelease`, `lunge`, and, as the default for the seven members the first version did not name, `waitMs`, `strikeHeft`, `moveTo`, `leap`, `strike` and `setFacing` (a part's action motion is the figure's own, and the same path brings the figure home at the action's end; the game's pods have no motion of their own in the girls' list, GR gap 2, so this is `[ours]`);
- **takes its position, centre and head points from a source that depends on Q-2**: under **(b)** from the stub, each part carrying its own region's anchor row, so the numbers, the cursor's box, a camera aim and a strike's reach land on the part's own anchor; under **(a)** from the figure, with each stub carrying a whole-figure anchor row (3.6), so the cursor's bracket is the figure's rectangle, the numbers land at the Special's one number point, and a camera aim and a strike's reach land on the figure;
- **swallows** `fadeTo`, `setAlpha`, `dissolveTo`, `setDissolve`, `setStone`, `setBrightness`, `lieDown`, `ramp` and `standDown` (a part never fades, dissolves, turns to stone, lies down or stands down; 21 of the port's 28 members were named before; the other seven, `waitMs`, `strikeHeft`, `moveTo`, `leap`, `strike`, `setFacing` and `standDown`, have the defaults stated here);
- leaves `project*`, `highlight` and `PartRings` on the stub (under (a) the stub's whole-figure row makes them answer for the figure; its ring is `hidden`).

### 4.5 Held rows during the change (engine gap G3)

The HUD draws one row per entry of the live state's `activeIds`, and the engine **assigns the new list to the live state object** (not to a copy) the moment a burst that contains the change is computed (`special/enter.ts`: `host.state.activeIds = [girl.id, ...pods]` on entry, the three girls again on exit), long before the presenter plays the burst. The first version said the held list could replace `activeIds` in the vitals projection with "no HUD code changes". **That does not hold (the code-fit check, C-2):** (a) `projectState` shares `activeIds` by design and is called only on the per-event vitals path (`syncVitals`); a full `syncHud` passes `engine.state()` itself, the live object, straight to `hud.sync`; (b) the HUD stores whichever object it was last handed as `lastState` (`sync`, line 882) and redraws its rows from it on three paths that never see the projection: `showSpherechange` ends with `renderParty(this.lastState, ...)` (so the rows would swap as the 800 ms flourish ends, not "at the first vitals event"), `onEvent('atb')` and `syncGauges` (a 20 Hz pump while a menu is open); and `lastState` is already the live, mutated object when the burst starts, so a copy handed to a later `sync` does not help until it arrives; (c) other readers of the live list during playback exist (the turn cut-in's "ATB n of m", `TurnCutIn.ts`; the lane fence in the HUD's own layout pass, which projects a head and feet for each id).

**The mechanism (second pass; the first amendment's wrapper `withSpecialPartyHold` is dropped, 14.2)** is the code-fit check's: an **optional `holdRows?(ids: readonly CombatantId[] | null): void` on `HudPort`** (additive; `HudPort.ts`) and, in `FFX2BattleHud.ts`, one field and one line in `renderParty`: `const ids = this.held ?? state.activeIds`. Every caller of `renderParty` then draws the held list whichever state object the HUD holds, with no copy and no wrapper: the flourish's closing redraw, the `atb` event, the gauge pump and every sync are all covered, which a wrapper over `sync`, `syncVitals` and `syncQueued` was not (it only chose what the HUD stored at the next sync, while `lastState` was already the mutated live object; it would also have needed a re-sync at the entry). The presenter-side holder (`SpecialPartyHold.ts`) sets the list at the entry, clears it in a `finally` and in `abort()`, and releases it with one **full `syncHud`** (the live object; it also re-seeds the vitals, `captureVitals`). **While the hold is up the Special's own beats call the full `syncHud`, not `syncVitals`**: `projectState` splices the pre-burst `hp` and `mp` over the live `stats.maxHp`, and the engine has already rescaled the main's pools on entry (`dressMain`), so a vitals sync would show the main's old HP over the form's new maximum until the burst's closing sync; a full sync shows the form's pools over the form's maxima (a test asserts the main's row text at the hold). With the flag off nothing calls `holdRows`, `held` stays null and `renderParty` runs as it does today; `FFX2BattleHud.ts` is 1,442 lines, over the cap, so the HUD side is a field and one line, nothing more. **The gauges** the held rows draw come from the HUD's stored snapshot, which changes only in `sync`, `onEvent('atb')` and `syncGauges`, so during the burst it is the old snapshot: the bars are the pre-change bars, frozen with the clock, which reads as halted time (not a frame reading: `[ours]`) (the first version's "an empty gauge because the snapshot is the new one" was wrong, code-fit m-2). The turn cut-in's slot count and the lane fence read `activeIds` through the state object, not through `renderParty`, and are covered by G17 (a snapshot test with a form up and with a hold set).

**Two more things read in the third amendment.** (1) **The name:** the call is `holdRows`, not `holdParty` (the code-fit check's name), because `SceneSlots.holdParty` already exists as a per-scene switch that keeps the party in place (`StageRelax.ts`, `fx/mix/staging.ts`) and a second `holdParty` with another meaning would collide in a codebase searched by grep. (2) **The mode chip is not shown outside a live target cursor**: `FFX2BattleHud.setActiveWaitVisible(on)` (private) shows and hides `.ffx2-atbmode`, and `setAtbMode` only repaints its text, so 2.1's "set through `setAtbMode`" would paint a hidden chip. Showing the chip for the sequence and the exit needs one more additive optional `HudPort` call (a `showAtbChip?(on)` that calls the private method; about +3 lines in `HudPort.ts` and +3 in `FFX2BattleHud.ts`, which is over its cap, so a call-in), set by the presenter-side holder with the hold and cleared with it in the same `finally` and in `abort()`; the chip's text is the HUD's own (`WAIT — ATB HELD` / `ACTIVE — ATB RUNNING`), not the game's "Wait Mode" and "Active Mode" words, `[ours]`.
