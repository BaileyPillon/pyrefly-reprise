# BATTLE CAMERA PERSPECTIVES — FFX, FFX-2, and comparable turn-based games

**Target project:** Pyrefly Reprise (Vite + TypeScript + Three.js, painted 2.5D billboards over 3D dioramas)
**Request quoted (Bailey, 2026-09-27):** (1) "I need very novel mock ups of how the game could look from different perspectives, right now the characters are turned a certain way toward enemies, explore all the possible ways it could look and which renders are likely to be perceived as quality and presentable and visually appealing eye candy to the player playing the game." (2) "also which one would similar games pick? which looks the most presentable and polished with the most eye candy? which one is most faithful and most canon? camera-moves-per-command idea? true-over-the-shoulder angle??? please elaborate and more mockups please."
**This document is the sourced research only.** It does not answer Bailey, propose mockups, or recommend a pick — that is the main session's job (hard rule 9/10). Everything below is text-sourced words about cameras, never a retail frame, screenshot, or model (rule 8).
**Research date:** 2026-09-27.
**Game case (AGENTS.md rule 14):** §A is FFX only, §B is FFX-2 only. §C–§D are reference material — how *other* games solved the same problem — and apply to **both** games' camera decisions equally; nothing in them is FFX/FFX-2 game data. §E is a summary of all of the above.
**Read first, not redone here:** `research/ffx-vs-ffx2-presentation.md` §0 (tags), §2 (battle intro — camera not player-controllable, reframes per action, no opening sweep), §9 row 6 (the opening-sweep verdict) — cited below, not re-litigated. `research/ff7-battle-staging.md` §0–1 (FF7's own camera/screen-side findings, used in §C as the FF7 row). `research/ffx2-combat-core.md` (free-movement/chaining mechanics, cited in §B).
**Companion facts already in the corpus, cited not repeated:** `visual-bible.md` §2.0/§6.3 — our **own build's** current battle camera (low three-quarter behind-and-to-one-side, distance/pitch numbers) is tagged `[estimate]` there: **"no source publishes FFX's camera parameters."** `docs/ART-PIPELINE.md` §2a — our own build currently stages **the party on the left, facing right; enemies on the right, facing left**, also `[ours]`, not sourced to canon. Both of these are *our* choices, not findings; §A and §E below explain why neither is contradicted or confirmed by canon, and where they actually stand against the one sourced datapoint that exists.

## A. Final Fantasy X

### A.1 Player control — confirmed absent, four independent times

No Config entry, no button, on PS2 or HD Remaster:

| Source | Quote / finding |
|---|---|
| GameFAQs, *Battle Camera Angles* (topic 48588178, 2009) | Q: "Is there a way to rotate the camera angle in battle/select an alternate one?" A: "There's no way to change any of the camera angles, unfortunately." |
| GameFAQs, *Is there a way to control the camera in battle?* (topic 45430529, 2008) | "I swore in battles you could press some button... haven't read anything about controlling the camera either." Reply: "I wish. No." |
| GameFAQs, *Why did they make FFX have an overhead view instead of a behind-the-back camera?* (topic 71327849, ~2015) | Mr_Hangman: "I don't think it's accurate to say it used an 'overhead view' instead of 'behind-the-back'. Rather the camera angle is chosen automatically and has various angles at various locations, rather than being player controlled." |
| FF Wiki, *Menu (Final Fantasy X)* (revid 4032396 for the battle-system page; Menu page revid 3724833) | The Config section lists subtitles, cursor default/memory, and Aeons Short/Full. **No camera entry of any kind.** |

`[verified: 4 sources]`. This corroborates and extends `ffx-vs-ffx2-presentation.md` §2.1's existing citation of a Steam thread and a GameFAQs thread to the same effect — four more independent hits, all "no."

### A.2 The camera is authored and reframes, but *when* is barely described

- "The camera angle is chosen automatically and has various angles at various locations" `[single source: GameFAQs Mr_Hangman, above]`.
- "Squaresoft used the camera angle to good effect. Whenever it swings around, it's usually significant and often reveals something" `[single source: GameFAQs falconesque, topic 71327849]`.
- Contradicted in tone (not fact) by another poster in the same thread: "This game is like a movie, the camera angles are fixed. I think is a good experience" `[single source: GameFAQs Calmlander]`. Read together, the two posts are compatible: each **shot** holds still (Calmlander's "fixed"), but the game **cuts between shots** on meaningful beats (falconesque's "swings around") — which is exactly `ffx-vs-ffx2-presentation.md` §2.1's already-sourced "constant authored motion... reframes per action rather than sitting on a locked-off master."
- **None of this distinguishes (a) command-picking, (b) the party member's own swing, (c) an enemy's swing, or (d) Overdrives/summons as different camera behaviours.** The FF Wiki's *Final Fantasy X battle system* (revid 4032396), *Aeon (Final Fantasy X)* (revid 4029264), and *Overdrive (Final Fantasy X)* (revid 4014432) pages were read in full for this: all three describe **what happens**, in words, and **never how the camera shows it** — image captions only ("Battle in the PS2 Final Fantasy X release. Note the Act List on the right"). This is `[absence]` on all four sub-questions, not silence I'm inferring from — it's checked and confirmed absent in the wiki's own text.
- The one sub-question with a real, sourced mechanical fact: **aeons are not a cutscene.** "Aeons differ from previous summons in the Final Fantasy series by being controllable by the player once summoned. Summons being directly controllable as opposed to being mere cut scenes has since continued in later games" `[single source: FF Wiki, Aeon (Final Fantasy X), revid 4029264]`. So an aeon's **entrance** is the one-off spectacle beat (glyph on the ground, aeon emerges through it, `[verified: 2 sources]` already in `ffx-vs-ffx2-presentation.md` §7), but once she's in, she is framed like **any other acting party member**, not held in a special "summon cam" — `[derived]` from the "controllable, not a cutscene" sentence above.

### A.3 Screen side — genuinely unattested for this game

Checked directly and came up empty: no FFX-specific source (wiki, GameFAQs, review) states which side of the screen the party stands on. The only sourced datapoint in the whole series is FF1 — "*Final Fantasy* was the first game to show the player's characters on the right side of the screen and the enemies on the left side of the screen" `[single source: Wikipedia, Final Fantasy (video game); verified against the live article text on 2026-09-27]` — which `research/ff7-battle-staging.md` §2 already independently, informally corroborates as continuing through FF7 (GameFAQs board + GamesRadar, both informal). **The Wikipedia article does not say the convention continues to FFX**, and I did not find anything else that does either. `[absence]`, stated plainly rather than assumed.

Practical consequence for the build: our own `docs/ART-PIPELINE.md` §2a stages **party left, enemies right** — the reverse of the one sourced datapoint (FF1) and its informal FF7 corroboration. That is not a "bug" (FFX itself is unattested), but it means the current default cannot be defended as "faithful" either; it is `[ours]`, same as the camera parameters in `visual-bible.md` §2.0.

### A.4 The one named-developer quote found — and its real scope

Yusuke Naora, FFX Art Director, in a 2001 developer interview: he explains the team **deliberately did not** build a camera that continuously follows the party, because "if the camera is always centered on and following the player characters, it can induce motion sickness" — something he'd personally experienced as a player and didn't want to inflict `[single source: shmuplations.com, "Final Fantasy X – 2001 Developer Interviews," Naora section]`. **This is stated in the context of the field/exploration camera** ("we put a lot of effort into the camera and camera angles this time too, as it's a tool for exploration") — Naora does not say the word "battle" in the quoted passage. Extending it to explain *why the battle camera also cuts rather than tracks* is `[derived]`, plausible (same team, same stated principle, same era) but not itself sourced. No other interview material was found that names the battle camera specifically — a search of the FFX 20th Anniversary interview series (frontlinejp.net, Part 2/4) turned up Tsuchida discussing party size and encounter-frequency math, nothing about the camera `[checked, absence]`.

## B. Final Fantasy X-2

### B.1 Free battle positions and back attacks — real and mechanically load-bearing

- "Free battle positions can be exploited with having a character perform a physical ability behind the target to strike for double damage" `[single source: FF Wiki, Active Time Battle, revid 3995539]`. This is the wiki's own contrast sentence for what's *new* in FFX-2's ATB versus earlier games.
- "Positioning drives chaining (this is why FFX-2's free-movement battlefield exists)... three characters standing close to one target land their attacks nearly simultaneously and chain easily; a character standing far away spends ~2 s running in, which usually breaks the chain" `[single source: Split Infinity's mechanics FAQ, cited in research/ffx2-combat-core.md §1 line 264]`. Long-range dresspheres (Gunner, Lady Luck, Alchemist, Trainer, Gun Mage) fire with no run-in at all `[verified: 2 sources, same file]`.
- The battlefield draws this state, not just tracks it internally: "the current target gets a white circle on the ground beneath it plus a yellow downward-pointing triangle above it... given the free-roaming positioning, this is load-bearing UI" `[single source, research/ffx2-combat-core.md §9]`.
- Player control over *where* a character stands is minimal to none in practice: "you can't control character positioning in X and X-2"; some AoE attacks (a breath attack's arc, for instance) can be partially avoided by placement, and the rare Psychic ability grants limited repositioning, but otherwise it's automatic `[single source, informal: Steam discussion, "Position in battle?"]`.

### B.2 The camera itself — no player control, and criticised for it

- Same absence of a Config entry as FFX: the *Menu (Final Fantasy X-2)* Config list is "Spherechanges, ATB Mode and Speed, Cursor, Battle Help, Vibration, Subtitles, Subtitle Names, Guide Map, Sound, Screen Position" `[single source: FF Wiki, Menu (Final Fantasy X-2), revid 3736537]` — no item plainly named "Camera." **"Screen Position" is the one ambiguous entry**: the wiki names it but never explains what it adjusts, and it sits in a group with Sound/Subtitles/Vibration — display-calibration-shaped company, not gameplay-shaped. Read as PS2-era overscan/TV-alignment, not a battle camera control, but that reading is `[derived]`, not stated anywhere; the actual behaviour is `[unsourced]`, exactly as `ffx-vs-ffx2-presentation.md` §11 item 7 already flagged for the *values*, not just this specific entry.
- Player-facing complaints, informal but directly on-topic: "the camera angles are always complete ass and I almost can never see anything"; another player calls it "the jankiest battle system I've ever seen"; a third specifically cites losing track of a Limit Break because "the camera inside was awful" in a corridor (the *Fahrenheit* airship's confined interiors) `[single source, informal: Steam discussion, "FFX2 Battle Camera"]`. Consistent complaint shape: **not that the player lacks control** (nobody expects to have any) but that the automatic framing handles tight spaces worse than open ones.

### B.3 Spherechange and the three girls moving — thin beyond what the corpus already has

- Time halts and the party is invulnerable during a spherechange, and it plays in full the first time then abbreviates per the Config toggle — both already sourced in `ffx-vs-ffx2-presentation.md` §6. **No source describes the camera's behaviour during the sequence** — does it hold, orbit, push in? `[absence]`, matching that document's own §11 item 12.
- "The girls" moving as a trio: beyond the free-positioning/back-attack facts in B.1, no source describes how the camera frames three independently-moving party members as a group (does it stay wide to hold all three, or cut to whichever one is acting?). `[absence]`.

## D. Reception — what critics credit for the best-looking turn-based battles

**Clair Obscur: Expedition 33** — "universal acclaim" per Metacritic, 98% critic recommendation on OpenCritic `[verified: 2 sources]`. What's specifically credited: "every scene looks like a painting, with dramatic contrasts of brightness and shadow"; "the lighting, environmental storytelling, and cinematic framing give the impression that each shot has been composed with intention, like a still from an art film"; animation singled out as beyond what "studios of over 500 people" typically manage `[verified: 2 sources — thejimquisition.com; joshcv.substack.com, aggregated via OpenCritic]`. The over-the-shoulder camera is the one specific point of *complaint* inside the acclaim — motion sickness reports and a community camera mod `[single source: caniplaythat.com]`. **Net finding: the praise is about lighting, composition and animation, not the over-the-shoulder camera itself, which is the most contested part of the presentation.**

**Persona 5 Royal** — credited for turning combat into performance: "players don't just win battles – they perform them"; snappy animation, instant follow-ups, and the Showtime finishers as the visual high point `[single source: RPG Site review]`. The style is explicitly cited as the ancestor of newer titles' presentation ambitions, including a direct claim that P5's combat style influenced Clair Obscur's own sequel-in-development `[single source: PSU.com]`.

**Octopath Traveler II / HD-2D** — the term itself is Square Enix's trademarked name for "retro Super NES-style character sprites and textures with polygonal environments and high-definition effects" `[single source: Wikipedia, HD-2D, cross-referenced against TechRaptor's trademark-filing report]`. The mechanism specifically credited for making it read as a diorama rather than a collage: **tilt-shift** — "a tilt-shift camera setup with a wide field of view that flattens the viewpoint while keeping depth," combined with dynamic lighting and depth-of-field blur on out-of-focus elements `[single source, synthesized from multiple outlets — see Sources below]`. This is the same mechanism `visual-bible.md` §6.3 already implements for Pyrefly Reprise (screen-space band blur, explicitly *not* rotation and *not* depth-based DOF) — worth noting the parallel is real, not just a naming coincidence.

**Where flat sprites-in-3D-space go wrong — "cardboard."** The literal word "cardboard" was found describing 2D **scenery** (trees) rather than characters: rotating the camera in some pseudo-3D games makes foliage "look more like pairs of intersecting cardboard cutouts" `[single source, informal: ResetEra thread, "2D sprites in 3D environments"]`. The same thread's broader history is useful precedent: Grandia (1997) put 2D character sprites on flat 2D battle backgrounds inside a 3D-navigable world; Breath of Fire IV is credited as having "basically perfected the marriage of 2D assets in a 3D world," to the point it's "really difficult to tell where one element ends and the other begins" `[single source, same thread]`. Octopath Traveler itself draws a **different**, resolution-shaped criticism, not a "flat cardboard" one: "the blocky pixel edges of sprite characters and objects clash against the otherwise clean and crisp 3D edges," compared to how low-res sprites look wrong in an upscaled emulator `[single source: ResetEra, "So am I the only one who hates the new 2D-HD graphics?"]`. **No source was found using "cardboard" for a billboarded character specifically** (as opposed to background props) in the games searched — this stays `[absence]`, not a confirmed risk, though it is exactly the failure mode `docs/ART-PIPELINE.md` §2a already names by instinct ("a flat cutout in profile reads as cardboard on the battlefield") when it rejects strict 90° profiles.
