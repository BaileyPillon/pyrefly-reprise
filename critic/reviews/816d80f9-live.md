## Findings (all informational; none critical or major)

**LV39-1 (informational; a handoff sentence, FFX-2 only).** While Lady Luck's reels are open, **P does not pause**: `BattleScreen.canPause` returns false whenever the playback phase names a minigame (the live snapshot read `canPause: false`, phase `hud:minigame-request`), and its comment says so ("A minigame still blocks it: those are timed inputs"). P pressed over the open reels left the screen at `battle` and the timer bar went on (10.8 % to 17.6 % over the 0.8 s that followed). `docs/handoff/ladyluck-reels-a.md` says "a pause freezes the reels and the timer"; the overlay's own code and unit tests do that when `rawInputSuspended()` is true, but in the shipped game the keyboard can never reach that state over the reels (a debug beat could). So nothing can be lined up by pausing, the 12 s safety timer and the 5-symbols-a-second strip are as built, and no player is hurt; the sentence in the handoff (or the intent behind it) is what needs a look. Suggested, not tried: correct the handoff, or decide whether a pause should be allowed there.

**LV39-2 (hosting; carried from the release-38 live pass LV-1; Bailey's decision, not a product change).** The HTML a browser receives still carries Cloudflare Web Analytics' one injected script (366 bytes plus a line feed now; 367 before): a `type="module"` script from `static.cloudflareinsights.com` with an integrity hash and a `data-cf-beacon` config. Every page load fetches it (200) and POSTs to `/cdn-cgi/rum` (204): one third-party host and a telemetry beacon per visit (10 and 10 over 10 loads here). `verify-live` now fetches the page with a browser's `Accept` and tolerates exactly this one element (D-418), which is why PASS stands and why the beacon is reported rather than hidden. It does not touch play (0 console errors).

**LV39-3 (coverage note).** In 50-to-85-second sessions at 1600x900 and on the phone the game asked for no `@2x`, `@3x` or `@4x` master at all (0 requests with `@` in any form in every session but 7), so the "carry `%40`, no 307" rule had nothing to check by itself. It was proved with the documented `?artscale=2` pin (session 7: 88 requests, all `%40`, all 200, none redirected), which exercises the same URL builder; the natural path (a long fight where the art governor upgrades a figure to its @2x master) was not seen and is under notTested. (The release-38 pass met 32 x 307 on literal-`@` requests; none here.)

**LV39-4 (informational; from the build).** The ANGLE shader-compile warning X3595 ("gradient instruction used in a loop with varying iteration") from `THREE.WebGLProgram` appears in each of the two phone sessions (the same text as release 38's LV-5, there too only on the phone); no desktop session logged any warning. A warning, not an error; the frames are fine.

**LV39-5 (observation, FFX only; not a defect).** The Sensor card at Natus's first menu has two states and a thin margin worth knowing for the deep review. It is open for the first few seconds (box 297x223 at x 1001 to 1299, y 8 to 230, but its drawn panel ends at y 209) and then folds to a single header line at the top. His left wing tip rises as high as y 224 as he breathes, so against the drawn panel the margin is 15 to 20 px while the card is open and 179 to 188 px once it has folded; the tip does enter the last 6 px of the card's unseen DOM box (bottom 230.5), where nothing is drawn. 0 of 14 paired frames overlap and the product measures 0 under HUD. With the cursor on Natus the card stayed at the pinned place (x 1001 to 1299) in the counted run, while in an earlier superseded run of the same flow it had steered 91 px to the left (CSS left 318.4 px, x 772 to 1070); both are above him and neither covers him. The focused review's R39F-11 (the card touching the tips at 1280x720 and 1366x768) is a different window size and was not re-measured here.

**LV39-6 (method notes).** (a) A fresh profile's first battle of each chapter shows the first-run guide line (and, in Ch I, a first-time Overdrive card at Kimahri's menu), and a bare Enter only takes such a line down (PR-0362, by design): "Enter, Enter" does not attack on the first menu, and the harness presses the extra Enter by reading `.coach-layer`.
